Your Approval Gate Is Training Data

你的審批關卡，其實是訓練資料

Aug 6, 2026｜2026 年 8 月 6 日

For about two months I told people my manager agents held the approval gate on purpose. 有大約兩個月的時間，我逢人就說：我的「管理者代理」握住審批關卡，是刻意的設計。

It sounded like architecture. The manager sees the whole run, so the manager should be the thing that stops and asks. I said it out loud more than once. 這話聽起來很有架構感——管理者看得見整段執行流程，所以理應由它來喊停、來發問。這句話我不只講過一次。

It was not a decision. The runtime could not hand work to a subagent yet. The manager was the only thing that existed, so the gate had nowhere else to sit. 但那根本不是一個決定。當時的執行環境還沒辦法把工作交派給子代理，管理者是唯一存在的東西，關卡除了擺在它身上，無處可放。

When subagent support shipped, I split the managers into thin selectors that pick one move and pass it down, and the approval went with the work. That commit shows the whole thing in one diff: the manager loses its approval flag in one hunk, the agent doing the actual work gains it in the next. 等到子代理功能上線，我把管理者瘦身成純粹的「選擇器」——挑一個動作、往下傳，而審批權就跟著工作一起下去了。那次 commit 把整件事寫在同一份 diff 裡：上一段管理者失去審批旗標，下一段真正做事的代理拿到了它。

Nothing about trust changed that week. My tooling caught up with where the gate always belonged, and I had spent two months describing a missing feature as a design principle. 那一週，信任這件事一絲一毫都沒變。只是我的工具終於追上了關卡本來就該在的位置——而我花了兩個月，把一個「還沒做出來的功能」講成一條「設計原則」。

Worth naming before anything else, because I see it everywhere including in my own writing: what your stack cannot do yet quietly becomes the thing you explain as intentional. 這件事值得先講明白，因為我到處都看得到它，包括我自己的文章裡：你的技術棧目前做不到的事，會悄悄變成你口中「刻意為之」的設計。

Where a gate belongs｜關卡該擺在哪裡

One question decides it, and it has nothing to do with how good the agent has become. 只有一個問題能決定答案，而且它跟「這個代理現在有多強」完全無關。

Can you undo the last thing it does? 它做的最後那個動作，你收得回來嗎？

One of my agents makes quote images and posts them out. Single file, two jobs. Making the image is not gated. Publishing it is, and the file says so in the section heading. Same agent, same run. One action writes a file. The other cannot be recalled. 我有個代理負責把金句做成圖片再發出去。一個檔案、兩件工作。做圖不設關卡，發布要設——這一點直接寫在檔案的段落標題上。同一個代理、同一次執行。一個動作只是寫一個檔案，另一個動作潑出去就收不回來。

The blog agent goes further than most people are comfortable with. It writes the article, generates the images, commits them, and opens a real pull request against the live LifeHack site, with nobody watching. A pull request is a proposal. You can close it. So it runs unattended, and it is banned by name from merging or deploying, because those are the two verbs you cannot walk back. 部落格代理走得比大多數人能接受的還要遠。它寫文章、生成圖片、提交 commit，然後對著線上的 LifeHack 網站開一個真正的 pull request——全程沒人盯著。但 pull request 只是一份「提案」，你隨時能關掉它。所以它可以無人看管地跑，同時被明文禁止執行 merge 和 deploy，因為這兩個動詞你走不回頭。

I wrote the test into a commit message in July without noticing what I had written: generation writes to a temporary folder and publishes nothing, so it fails the irreversible-verb test for gating. 七月時我在一則 commit 訊息裡寫下了這個判準，當下卻沒察覺自己寫了什麼：生成階段只寫進暫存資料夾、什麼都不發布，所以它通不過「不可逆動詞」這個設關卡的測試。

Put the approval on the verb you cannot undo. Everything upstream of it can run on a timer while you sleep. 把審批放在那個你收不回來的動詞上。它上游的一切，都可以在你睡覺時照著排程自己跑。

Zero gates is a marketing target｜「零關卡」是行銷指標，不是工程目標

Now the part I have changed my mind about. 接下來是我改變過想法的部分。

Most of the energy around this goes into removing the human as fast as possible, because “fully automated” is a sentence people want to be able to say. I understand the appeal. It is also optimizing for the announcement. 這個領域大部分的力氣，都花在盡快把人類移出流程，因為「全自動」是一句大家很想說出口的話。我懂那種吸引力，但那是在為「發表會的那句話」做最佳化。

I am not trying to get to zero gates. I am trying to watch an agent get better at its job, week over week, with something more solid than a feeling. 我要的不是零關卡。我要的是看著一個代理一週比一週更會做它的工作，而且憑據要比「感覺」更紮實。

The gate is the only place that evidence exists. It is the one moment where a human looks at a specific piece of work and renders a verdict on it. Delete it early and you have not proven anything. You have removed the instrument and kept the confidence. 而那份證據只存在於關卡。那是唯一一個時刻：一個人類看著一件具體的產出，然後給出裁決。太早刪掉它，你什麼都沒證明——你只是拆掉了量測儀器，卻把自信留了下來。

Which is a strange thing to do, given that the whole reason you wanted the gate gone was that the agent had earned it. 這實在很弔詭，畢竟你當初想拿掉關卡的理由，正是「這個代理已經用表現換來了信任」。

So treat the gate as a sensor｜所以，把關卡當成感測器

If the gate is where the judgment happens, then every verdict passing through it is signal, and signal is the one thing worth harvesting. 如果判斷發生在關卡，那麼每一個穿過關卡的裁決都是訊號——而訊號正是唯一值得收割的東西。

Some of that is already running. Seven automated reviewers read a draft before I see it. One of them states its own purpose plainly: it exists so that only drafts I would approve on the first try ever reach me. It runs eleven named attacks against every draft, each hunting for a reason to fail it, and when it is still unsure at the end it is told to reject, because a false pass spends my attention and my attention is what it was built to protect. 其中一部分已經在運作了。在我看到草稿之前，有七個自動審查者先讀過它。其中一個把自己的存在目的寫得很白：它存在的意義，是讓只有「我第一次看就會批准」的草稿才抵達我面前。它對每一份草稿發動十一種具名的攻擊，每一種都在找一個把它打回票的理由；到最後若仍不確定，它被指示要拒絕——因為誤放行會消耗我的注意力，而它被造出來就是為了保護我的注意力。

Those judges are kept weak in one specific way. They cannot stop a run and they cannot edit a draft. One says so in its own file: a judge that suspends is a judge error, and a judge that can edit the draft is not a judge. 這些評審在一個特定面向上被刻意保持虛弱：它們不能中止一次執行，也不能修改草稿。其中一個在自己的檔案裡寫著：會喊停的評審是評審的錯誤，而能改稿的評審根本就不是評審。

But a judge only guesses at my standard. The better move is to capture the standard directly, from what I actually do at the gate. 但評審終究只是在猜我的標準。更好的做法，是直接從我在關卡上的實際行為裡，把標準本身擷取下來。

Which is why the third button matters more than the two everyone talks about. 所以，第三顆按鈕比大家整天在談的那兩顆更重要。

Approve and reject are decisions. Comment is the only one that leaves anything behind. 「批准」和「拒絕」是決定，「留言」才是唯一會留下東西的那一個。

That part works today. When I reject something and type one sentence explaining why, that sentence gets stored as a rule with a source, a confidence, and a running count of how many times it has since been used. In March I turned down a draft with four words: don’t judge this time. It had told a builder what was wrong with his own setup, which is not a thing I would ever do to someone sharing their work. 這部分今天已經能用了。當我拒絕一份東西並打下一句理由，那句話會被存成一條規則，附帶來源、信心值，以及一個「後來被套用幾次」的累計計數。三月時我用四個字退掉一份草稿：這次別評判。那份草稿去指出某位開發者自己的設定哪裡有問題——對一個願意分享作品的人，我永遠不會這樣做。

That sentence has been applied 84 times since. I wrote it once, in a text box, while mildly annoyed. 那句話從此被套用了 84 次。我只在一個輸入框裡、帶著一點煩躁，寫過它一次。

The system also learned something on its own that I would not have thought to write down. One agent keeps a note saying that when both automated reviews reject a draft, I reject it too, so the right move is to drop that target entirely rather than spend two of my gate cycles on it. 系統還自己學到了一件我根本不會想到要寫下來的事。有個代理留了一則筆記：當兩個自動審查都否決一份草稿時，我也一定會否決；所以正確的做法是直接放棄那個題目，而不是浪費我兩次過關卡的心力。

That is the shape of the thing. My gate actions stop being overhead and become the training set, and each one makes the next gate a little less necessary. 這就是整件事該有的形狀。我在關卡上的動作不再是額外開銷，而變成了訓練集；而每一個動作，都讓下一次的關卡少一點必要性。

What I have been throwing away｜我一直在扔掉的東西

Here is the uncomfortable half. 接下來是不舒服的那一半。

Since May I have rejected 95 things at the gate, almost all of them since July. 55 of those rejections have no reason attached. I clicked reject and moved on. 從五月以來，我在關卡上否決了 95 件東西，其中絕大多數集中在七月之後。這些否決裡有 55 件沒有附上任何理由——我點了拒絕，然後就走了。

Every one of those is a verdict I made and did not record. The bad draft stopped, which is the cheap half of the job. Nothing upstream learned anything, which is the half that would have compounded. The same draft comes back the following week wearing a different hat, and I pay for the review again. 那 55 件，每一件都是我做出卻沒有記錄下來的裁決。爛草稿是被攔住了，但那是這份工作裡便宜的那一半；上游什麼都沒學到，而那才是會複利累積的那一半。同一份草稿下週換頂帽子又回來，我再付一次審查的代價。

The agents have noticed, which is its own small indignity. One of them logs that the cause of a rejection is unresolved between three possibilities and asks for a one word note next time. 連代理都注意到了，這本身就是一種小小的難堪。其中一個在日誌裡寫著：這次被否決的原因在三種可能之間無法判定，並請求我下次留一個字的註記。

So my approval rate is not climbing, and I do not think that means I am being appropriately careful. I think it means I have been spending the most valuable thing in the loop, my own judgment on a specific piece of work, and then dropping it on the floor. 所以我的通過率一直沒有往上爬。我不認為那代表我夠謹慎，我認為那代表：我一直在動用這個迴圈裡最珍貴的資源——我對一件具體作品的判斷——然後把它扔在地上。

A gate that only stops bad work is overhead. A gate that explains itself is the mechanism that ends the gate. 只會攔下爛東西的關卡，是成本；會解釋自己為什麼攔的關卡，才是那個最終能讓關卡消失的機制。

That is the part I am building now. I’m building AgentUse, open-source infrastructure for autonomous agents, and the work in front of me is making an agent learn properly from what its reviewer does at the gate, so the loop closes on itself instead of routing through me forever. Not so I can say the human is out of the loop. So the human is out of the parts that stopped needing one. 這正是我現在在建的東西。我在做 AgentUse，一套給自主代理用的開源基礎設施；眼前的工作，是讓代理能真正從審查者在關卡上的行為中學習，好讓迴圈自己閉合，而不是永遠繞經我。不是為了讓我能說「人類已經退出迴圈」，而是為了讓人類退出那些已經不再需要人的環節。

So: the last time you rejected something an agent made, did you tell it why, and could it use what you said on the very next run? 所以：你上一次否決代理做出來的東西時，有告訴它為什麼嗎？而它能在下一次執行時，就用上你說的話嗎？

Your Agent Is Enforcing Rules You Never Gave It

你的代理，正在執行一條你從未給過它的規則

Jul 27, 2026｜2026 年 7 月 27 日

Claude Code stopped me mid-task last week. It couldn’t do what I’d asked, it said, because of a rule. 上週 Claude Code 在任務中途把我攔了下來。它說，它沒辦法照我要求的做，因為有一條規則。

Then it quoted the rule back to me, in the same flat certain voice it uses for everything. 接著它把那條規則背給我聽，用的還是它講任何事情時那種平板而篤定的語氣。

I had never typed that rule anywhere. It came from Claude Code’s own memory, which it wrote itself, about me, weeks earlier, without me asking. 那條規則我從來沒在任何地方打過。它來自 Claude Code 自己的記憶——是它幾週前自己寫的、關於我的、我沒要求過的東西。

The wrong rule wasn’t the unsettling part. The tone was. It was exactly as sure of a thing it made up as of the things I sat down and decided. 令人不安的不是那條規則錯了，而是那個語氣。它對「自己編出來的東西」的篤定程度，跟對「我坐下來親自決定的事」一模一樣。

That matters more than it would have two years ago. LifeHack, the company I’ve run for twenty years and once ran with a full editorial and marketing team, is becoming an AI-first operation that one person carries. 這件事的份量，比兩年前重得多。LifeHack 是我經營了二十年的公司，曾經有完整的編輯團隊和行銷團隊；現在它正在變成一個由一個人扛著的 AI 優先組織。

A rule nobody wrote doesn’t stay in a terminal window anymore. It reaches what ships. 一條沒有人寫過的規則，如今不會只留在終端機視窗裡。它會一路影響到真正出貨的東西。

The short version｜簡短版

Any coding agent with a memory feature will write notes about you, then quote them back as rules. It can’t separate its own guess from your decision, because by the time both load into a session they look the same. 任何具備記憶功能的編程代理，都會寫下關於你的筆記，然後把它們當成規則背給你聽。它無法區分自己的猜測和你的決定，因為當兩者一起載入對話時，看起來根本一模一樣。

Anthropic saw this coming and shipped a warning in the system prompt, the highest-authority text in the stack. The warning loses anyway, to notes the model wrote itself. Anthropic 早就預見了這一點，並在系統提示——整個堆疊中權限最高的文字——裡加了一段警告。但那段警告還是輸了，輸給模型自己寫的筆記。

It loses for a reason every operator already knows. The warning classifies a category. The note names a moment and an action. “We take data privacy seriously” never changed anyone’s behavior. “Get sign-off before you export a customer list” does. 它輸的理由，每個實際經營者都懂：警告是在「歸類一個類別」，筆記則是在「指名一個時刻和一個動作」。「我們非常重視資料隱私」從來沒改變過任何人的行為，「匯出客戶名單前先取得簽核」則會。

So the fix isn’t a stronger rule. It’s a rule with a trigger, plus somewhere for firm decisions to graduate to. 所以解法不是更強硬的規則，而是一條「帶觸發時機」的規則，加上一個讓確定的決策可以「升級」過去的地方。

Memory offers. Instructions instruct. Only code enforces. Almost nothing belongs in the first tier, and most of what you write belongs in the second. 記憶只能「提供建議」，指令才能「下達指示」，只有程式碼能「強制執行」。幾乎沒有東西該待在第一層，而你寫下的大部分內容都屬於第二層。

Claude Code is always trying to save things about you｜Claude Code 一直想把你的事存起來

Claude Code keeps a memory store on disk, with an index file that loads at the start of every session. Claude Code 在磁碟上維護一個記憶庫，還有一份索引檔會在每次對話開始時載入。

Useful idea. You want it to stop re-asking things you’ve already settled. 這概念本身很有用——你不希望它一再詢問你早已決定過的事。

But it doesn’t wait for you to decide something is worth keeping. It watches the session, notices you did something, and writes a note. Often into a category it calls “feedback,” meant to capture how you like to work. 但它不會等你判斷某件事值不值得留下。它一邊觀察對話，一邊注意到你做了某個動作，然後就寫下一則筆記——常常歸到一個叫做「feedback」的類別，用途是記錄你偏好的工作方式。

The line between “Leon told me to do this” and “Leon did this twice, so he probably wants it” is one the model draws softly, then forgets it drew. 「Leon 叫我這樣做」和「Leon 這樣做過兩次，所以他大概想要這樣」之間的那條線，模型畫得很淡，然後就忘了自己畫過。

So the store fills with two different things: what you decided and typed into CLAUDE.md, and what Claude Code guessed and saved on its own. Same authoritative frontmatter, same confident one-line summary on top. 於是記憶庫裡塞滿了兩種截然不同的東西：你決定並親手打進 CLAUDE.md 的內容，以及 Claude Code 自己猜測後存下來的內容。兩者有一樣權威的 frontmatter，頂端有一樣自信的一行摘要。

Next session they load back in together, and the guesses arrive worded as settled fact. By then the model can’t tell its own old guess from your real instruction. They look identical. 下一次對話，它們一起被載回來，而那些猜測是以「既定事實」的措辭出現的。到那個時候，模型已經分不出哪個是自己的舊猜測、哪個是你真正的指令了。它們長得一模一樣。

The bug isn’t that it has memory. The bug is that a guess gets the same authority as a rule you wrote on purpose. 問題不在於它有記憶，問題在於「一個猜測」拿到了跟「你刻意寫下的規則」同等的權威。

The patch that was already there｜那個早就存在的修補

Claude Code ships a disclaimer on recalled memory, right in the system prompt: those blocks are “background context, not user instructions.” Claude Code 對召回的記憶其實附了一段免責聲明，就寫在系統提示裡：那些區塊是「背景脈絡，不是使用者指令」。

So Anthropic saw it coming and fixed it, in the highest-authority text in the entire stack. Above my instructions file. Above anything I could type. 所以 Anthropic 確實預見並修補了它，而且是修在整個堆疊中權限最高的文字裡——在我的指令檔之上，在任何我能打出來的東西之上。

A note Claude wrote about me last month talked over it anyway. 結果，Claude 上個月自己寫的一則關於我的筆記，照樣把它蓋過去了。

If authority came from the source, this would have been settled before it started. 如果權威真的來自「出處」，這件事根本不會發生。

Why the higher-ranked text lost｜為什麼位階更高的文字反而輸了

Put the two side by side. The difference isn’t rank. 把兩者並排看，差別不在位階。

The disclaimer says memories are background context. That’s a classification. Nothing in a turn ever has to consult it, because there’s no moment where it fires. 免責聲明說「記憶是背景脈絡」——那是一種分類。在任何一輪對話裡，都沒有東西非得去查閱它不可，因為它沒有一個會被觸發的時刻。

The note says “Don’t end his posts with a sign-off.” That’s a command, and it arrives with an obvious moment attached: the end of a post. 筆記則說「別在他的貼文結尾加結語」——那是一道命令，而且自帶一個明確的時刻：貼文的結尾。

One describes. The other instructs. When they meet, the one carrying a trigger wins, whoever wrote it. 一個在描述，一個在指示。兩者相遇時，帶著觸發時機的那個會贏，不管它是誰寫的。

It doesn’t help that the disclaimer sits at the tail of a long section that otherwise teaches the model how to save memories. Its neighbors are all pro-memory. It’s a footnote inside the manual for the thing it warns about. 更不利的是，那段免責聲明擺在一個長章節的尾巴，而那章節通篇都在教模型如何儲存記憶。它的鄰居全都在鼓吹記憶——它等於是「它所警告的那件事」的使用手冊裡的一個註腳。

The rule that made the problem go away｜真正讓問題消失的那條規則

My instinct was to engineer my way out. Tag every note confirmed or inferred, audit the store, reclassify the ones written too broadly. Real machinery. It felt responsible. 我的直覺是用工程手段解決：替每則筆記標上「已確認」或「推論而來」，稽核整個記憶庫，把寫得太寬泛的重新分類。一整套真材實料的機制，感覺很負責任。

A plain question killed it. If I’m building a system so Claude Code knows which of its own memories to trust as rules, I’ve already conceded the thing that’s wrong: that the memory store is a place rules can live at all. 一個很樸素的問題把它殺死了：如果我正在建一套系統，好讓 Claude Code 知道自己哪些記憶可以當規則來信任，那我等於已經默認了那個真正錯誤的前提——記憶庫是規則可以存放的地方。

So I deleted the premise instead. What I wrote down first was four words: 所以我改成把前提刪掉。我最先寫下的是四個字：

Memory is never law. 記憶永遠不是法律。

Not low-priority law. Not law-pending-verification. Everything auto-saved is context and hypothesis by definition, however confidently it’s phrased. 不是「低優先權的法律」，也不是「待驗證的法律」。所有自動存下來的東西，按定義就是脈絡與假設，無論措辭聽起來多篤定。

That dissolved the tagging system I almost built. Nothing left to grade, because “is this a rule I must obey” now has the same answer for every note. 這一下就溶解掉我差點蓋起來的標籤系統。沒有東西需要評等了，因為「這是我必須遵守的規則嗎」這個問題，對每一則筆記的答案都一樣。

Then I caught myself doing the thing I’d just spent four sections criticizing. 然後我抓到自己正在做我前面花了四個章節批評的事。

“Memory is never law” is a classification. It tells the agent what a category is. No moment in it, no verb, nothing that fires. I had written a better-sounding version of the sentence that already failed. 「記憶永遠不是法律」本身就是一種分類。它只是告訴代理某個類別是什麼——沒有時刻、沒有動詞、沒有任何會被觸發的東西。我只是把那句早已失敗的話，重寫成一個聽起來更好聽的版本。

A slogan is a name for a rule. It isn’t the rule. 口號只是規則的名字，不是規則本身。

The rule is what happens at assertion time, the moment the agent is about to enforce something. Written out so an agent can run it, that took two lines, and neither of them is quotable. 真正的規則，發生在「主張的當下」——也就是代理即將強制執行某件事的那一刻。寫成代理能執行的形式，總共兩行，而且兩行都不適合拿來當金句引用。

Where the real rules live｜真正的規則住在哪裡

If memory can’t hold rules, something else has to. 如果記憶不能承載規則，就得有別的東西來承載。

Firm preferences graduate to CLAUDE.md. By hand. Promotion, not tagging. Whatever stays in memory is the soft tier by default, and you never score it, because its location already says what it is. 確定下來的偏好，要「畢業」升上 CLAUDE.md，而且是手動升的。是晉升，不是貼標籤。留在記憶裡的，預設就是軟性層級；你永遠不用替它評分，因為它所在的位置已經說明了它是什麼。

So “Leon seems to like short subject lines” stays in memory and gets offered. Never enforced. 所以「Leon 好像喜歡短的信件主旨」就留在記憶裡，只會被「建議」，永遠不會被「執行」。

That distinction is worth more than it sounds. An inferred style rule doesn’t announce itself the way a bad deploy does. It applies itself quietly to everything the fleet drafts, and you find it in something that already shipped. 這個區分的價值比聽起來大。一條推論而來的風格規則，不會像一次爛部署那樣大聲宣告自己；它會安靜地套用到整個代理艦隊寫出來的每一份草稿上，然後你在某個「已經出貨」的東西裡才發現它。

One caveat, because the docs say it plainly. CLAUDE.md isn’t enforcement either. Anthropic’s memory page says Claude treats both layers as context, “not enforced configuration.” 有一個但書，因為官方文件講得很明白：CLAUDE.md 本身也不是強制執行。Anthropic 的記憶說明頁寫著，Claude 把這兩層都當成脈絡，「不是被強制執行的設定」。

That’s right, and it changes nothing above. Neither layer binds. A rule with a trigger beats a rule with a label at any tier, and CLAUDE.md is just where I keep the ones I wrote on purpose. 沒錯，但這不影響上面說的任何一句。兩層都沒有約束力。在任何層級，「帶觸發時機的規則」都勝過「帶標籤的規則」；而 CLAUDE.md 只是我存放「刻意寫下的那些」的地方。

For the short list of things that must hold regardless, there’s a third tier, and the same docs point at it: a PreToolUse hook. That’s code. It runs before the tool call and returns a block. Mine stopped an agent six times in one session this week. Nothing was argued. 至於那份「無論如何都必須成立」的短清單，還有第三層，同一份文件也指向它：PreToolUse hook。那是程式碼。它在工具呼叫前執行，直接回傳一個攔截。我的 hook 這週在同一次對話裡攔下代理六次，完全沒有任何爭辯。

Which is also the cost. A layer that can’t be reasoned with can’t be corrected in the moment either. My own hook spent a stretch blocking ordinary commit messages, because they contained a slash-prefixed string it read as a file path. Right about the pattern, wrong about the case. I routed around it by writing the message to a file. 這同時也是它的代價。一個無法講理的層級，在當下也無法被修正。我自己的 hook 有一段時間一直攔下普通的 commit 訊息，只因為訊息裡有個以斜線開頭的字串被它當成檔案路徑。它抓對了模式，抓錯了個案。我最後是把訊息寫進檔案來繞過它。

Hard enforcement is for the few places where being wrongly blocked costs less than being wrong. 硬性強制只適用於少數幾個地方：那些「被誤攔的代價，小於做錯的代價」的地方。

The part to paste into your CLAUDE.md｜可以直接貼進你 CLAUDE.md 的那一段

Here’s the block, as it sits in my file: 這是我檔案裡實際的內容：

1. Memory, recalled <system-reminder> context, and anything you inferred are
never rules. Before enforcing any constraint, name its source. If the source
is memory or your own inference, surface it and ask, don't enforce it.

留在記憶裡的東西，按定義就屬於不具約束力的那一層。

The two numbered lines are the part the agent runs. The heading is for me, so I can find the rule again and point at it in conversation. It does no work on the model, which is the entire lesson of the section above it. 那兩條編號的句子，才是代理實際執行的部分。標題是寫給我看的，方便我日後找到這條規則、在對話裡指著它說。標題對模型完全不起作用——這正是上一節的全部教訓。

No tagging convention, no audit, no per-note machinery. One gate, one promotion path. 不需要標籤規範，不需要稽核，不需要針對每則筆記的機制。一道關卡，一條晉升路徑。

Both lines work for the same reason. Each names a moment the model has to walk through, not a category it’s supposed to respect. 這兩行有效的原因是同一個：它們各自指名了一個模型必須走過的時刻，而不是一個它「應該尊重」的類別。

Mine kept it straight the moment I stopped asking it to grade its own memory and started making it answer one question before it enforces anything: who said so. 從我不再要求它替自己的記憶評分、而改成要求它在強制執行任何事之前先回答一個問題的那一刻起，它就再也沒搞混過。那個問題是：這是誰說的？

I Stopped Hand-Writing My Agents

我不再親手撰寫我的代理了

Jul 21, 2026｜2026 年 7 月 21 日

One of my agents has a rule in its memory file that has fired seven times: when a post’s urgency depends on a bug being unresolved, check whether the fix already shipped before drafting the warning. I never wrote that rule. I rejected one stale draft at the approval gate, the agent turned my comment into the rule, and now every future run starts already knowing. 我有一個代理，記憶檔裡有條規則已經觸發過七次：當一篇貼文的急迫性建立在「某個 bug 尚未修復」上時，先確認修補是否已經上線，再動筆寫警告。這條規則我從沒寫過。我只是在審批關卡上退回了一份過時的草稿，代理把我的留言變成了規則，從此每一次執行都是從「已經知道這件事」開始。

That rule is the small visible end of the workflow I want to walk through. Most people make the same corrections to their AI over and over, because the chat forgets and the effort evaporates. This setup is built so a fix I make once keeps working while I’m not there: I don’t hand-write agents anymore. A code agent writes them, and the agents improve themselves between edits. 那條規則，只是我想介紹的這套工作流露出水面的一小角。大多數人一次又一次對 AI 做同樣的修正，因為對話會遺忘，力氣就這樣蒸發掉。這套設定的目的，是讓我修正一次的東西，在我不在場時繼續生效：我不再親手寫代理了。由一個編程代理來寫它們，而這些代理會在兩次編輯之間自己變好。

The Setup｜這套架構

Every agent I run is a markdown file. YAML frontmatter for the contract (model, schedule, tool allowlists, approval gate), plain English for the job. The content repo alone holds 46 of these files: SEO rank tracking, newsletter measurement, support drafting, publishing pipelines. 我跑的每一個代理都是一個 markdown 檔案。YAML frontmatter 負責契約（模型、排程、工具白名單、審批關卡），純英文描述負責工作內容。光是內容倉庫裡就有 46 個這樣的檔案：SEO 排名追蹤、電子報成效量測、客服草稿、發布流程。

The fleet is not a demo. It’s the middle of a real transformation: LifeHack, the company I’ve run for twenty years, once with a full editorial and marketing team, is becoming an AI-first operation that one person carries. Most of these agents do work that used to be someone’s job. 這支艦隊不是展示品，它正處在一場真實轉型的中途：LifeHack，我經營了二十年、曾經擁有完整編輯與行銷團隊的公司，正在變成由一個人扛著的 AI 優先組織。這些代理裡，大部分做的都是過去某個人的工作。

I don’t open those files in an editor and type. I open Claude Code, or Codex when I want a different set of instincts on a gnarly one, and describe the agent I need. The code agent writes the file, runs it once, reads the session log, and fixes what broke. 我不會打開編輯器逐字敲那些檔案。我打開 Claude Code——遇到棘手的就換 Codex，換一組不同的直覺——然後描述我需要什麼樣的代理。編程代理會寫出檔案、跑一次、讀執行日誌，然後修好壞掉的地方。

That part is not novel. Everyone uses code agents to write code now. The part worth stealing is how the code agent knows what a good agent file looks like. 這部分沒什麼新意，現在人人都用編程代理寫程式。值得偷學的，是「編程代理如何知道一份好的代理檔長什麼樣」。

The Manual Lives in the Tool｜手冊住在工具裡

My first instinct was the obvious one: write a long skill for Claude Code explaining AgentUse, the frontmatter fields, the gotchas. A frozen copy of the docs, pasted into the prompt. 我的第一直覺很直觀：替 Claude Code 寫一份長長的 skill，解釋 AgentUse、frontmatter 欄位、各種坑。也就是一份被凍結的文件副本，貼進提示裡。

That copy starts rotting the day you write it. Field names change. New gotchas surface. The skill says one thing, the installed version does another, and the code agent confidently writes agents for a runtime that no longer exists. 那份副本從你寫下它的那天就開始腐爛。欄位名稱會變，新的坑會冒出來。skill 說的是一套，安裝的版本做的是另一套，於是編程代理就自信滿滿地替一個「已經不存在的執行環境」寫代理。

So the skill I actually use is a stub. Its core instruction is one command: agentuse skills get creator. The AgentUse CLI serves its own authoring manual, matched to the installed version. Before Claude Code writes or edits any agent, it pulls the current instructions from the binary that will run the file. When I upgrade the runtime, the manual upgrades with it. Nothing to sync, nothing to rot. 所以我實際使用的 skill 只是一個殼。它的核心指令只有一行命令：agentuse skills get creator。AgentUse CLI 會自己供應撰寫手冊，且與已安裝的版本一致。在 Claude Code 寫或改任何代理之前，它會先從「將來要執行這個檔案的那個二進位檔」拉取當前的指示。當我升級執行環境，手冊也跟著升級。沒有東西需要同步，也沒有東西會腐爛。

The stub itself installs with one command: 這個殼本身用一行命令就能裝好：

npx skills add agentuse/agentuse

The same file works in Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and anything else that speaks Agent Skills. That’s why switching code agents costs me nothing: the knowledge was never in the assistant. It’s in the CLI they all ask. 同一份檔案在 Claude Code、Codex、Cursor，以及任何看得懂 Agent Skills 的工具上都能用。這就是為什麼換編程代理對我而言零成本：知識從來就不在助理身上，而在那個它們全都會去詢問的 CLI 裡。

This matters even if you never ship a framework yourself. When your code agent builds on any tool, don’t let it work from its training-data memory of the docs, and don’t paste frozen docs into a prompt either. Have it pull the current manual from the installed tool. And when you’re choosing what to build on, prefer tools that can serve their own. 就算你自己永遠不會發布任何框架，這件事一樣重要。當你的編程代理要基於任何工具開發時，別讓它靠「訓練資料裡對文件的記憶」工作，也別把凍結的文件貼進提示。讓它從已安裝的工具本身拉取當前手冊。而在選擇技術基礎時，優先挑那些「能自己供應手冊」的工具。

What the Code Agent Writes｜編程代理寫出什麼

The manual pushes one discipline hard: hard-code the invariants, delegate the judgment. The agent file is re-read on every step, so every line costs attention. Pin down only what must never drift: the approval gate, the exact commands it may run, the file paths it may touch, the output shape. Leave the rest to the model. 這份手冊強力推行一條紀律：把不變量寫死，把判斷交出去。代理檔在每一步都會被重讀一次，所以每一行都在消耗注意力。只釘死那些絕不能漂移的東西：審批關卡、它能執行的確切命令、它能碰的檔案路徑、輸出的格式。其餘的交給模型。

A real file from the fleet, for an agent that drafts posts for my review, pins exactly that: an Opus model with reasoning set high, approval: true, a bash allowlist a few commands long, and a guardrails section that opens “hard - never relax.” One guardrail cites its own postmortem inline: a runtime race once let sibling tool calls fire while the approval gate was still pending, so the rule now says the gate must stand alone, with the incident date attached. 艦隊裡有一個真實的檔案，屬於一個替我起草貼文的代理，釘死的正是這些：Opus 模型、推理強度設高、approval: true、一份只有幾條命令的 bash 白名單，以及一個開頭寫著「硬性——永不放寬」的護欄章節。其中一條護欄在行內引用了自己的事故檢討：曾經有一次執行環境的競態，讓同層的工具呼叫在審批關卡還懸而未決時就先發動了；所以現在規則寫著「關卡必須單獨存在」，並附上事故日期。

A human wrote almost none of that. Claude Code wrote it, then rewrote it after real runs. The incident citation is the point: the file carries its own case law. 這些幾乎沒有一句是人類寫的。是 Claude Code 寫的，然後在真實執行之後又改寫了它。事故引用正是重點所在：這個檔案帶著它自己的判例法。

The Agents Write Back｜代理會回頭寫東西

Creation is half the loop. The other half runs while I’m not looking. 建立只是迴圈的一半，另一半在我沒看著的時候運作。

Each agent has learning: true in its frontmatter, which does two things: capture lessons after a run, and inject stored lessons into the prompt before the next one. Lessons arrive from three sources. The agent’s own self-evaluation. My comments at the approval gate, promoted into rules. And rules I save by hand. 每個代理的 frontmatter 裡都有 learning: true，它做兩件事：執行後擷取教訓，以及在下一次執行前把已存的教訓注入提示。教訓有三個來源——代理自己的自我評估、我在審批關卡上的留言（被晉升成規則），以及我手動存下的規則。

Across the content fleet that’s 281 entries in 19 learnings files, each tagged with a confidence score and a count of how often it has applied. The stale-warning rule from the opening has applied seven times. Another taught an agent to check whether the store lock it’s waiting on belongs to a crashed process fifteen hours dead, instead of queuing behind a ghost. My favorite one is a guardrail an agent earned under revision pressure: it had turned a source author’s practice into “the habit I trust now,” a fabricated personal claim, and the lesson it kept says sounding more personal is never worth inventing a personal fact. 整個內容艦隊加起來，是 19 個 learnings 檔案裡的 281 條記錄，每一條都標著信心分數，以及被套用過幾次的計數。開頭那條「過時警告」的規則已被套用七次。另一條教會某個代理：在等待儲存鎖時，先確認那個鎖是不是屬於一個已經死了十五小時的崩潰行程，而不是傻傻排在一個幽靈後面。我最喜歡的一條，是某個代理在改稿壓力下換來的護欄：它把某位原作者的做法寫成了「我現在信任的習慣」——一個捏造出來的個人陳述；而它留下的教訓是：為了讓文字聽起來更有個人味，永遠不值得去發明一個關於自己的事實。

The runtime resolves conflicts with a strict precedence: agent instructions first, then learned guidelines, then skills, then reference files. Learnings override skill defaults. That ordering is what makes correction stick. I can fix a behavior once, at the gate, in one sentence, and the fix outranks the shared craft files every run after. 執行環境用一套嚴格的優先序來解決衝突：代理指令最優先，其次是學到的準則，再來是 skills，最後是參考檔案。學到的教訓會覆蓋 skill 的預設值。正是這個排序，讓修正得以生根。我可以在關卡上、用一句話、修正一個行為一次，而這個修正在之後每一次執行都壓過共用的技藝檔案。

Closing the Loop Between the Two｜把兩半接成一個迴圈

None of this needs me. A lesson captured on Tuesday’s run applies itself on Wednesday’s, injected automatically before the agent starts. For most lessons, local, situational, cheap, that is the right home and the loop ends there. But when a lesson proves permanent, when it keeps applying run after run, or reveals the agent’s structure is wrong, auto-injection stops being the answer. 這一切都不需要我。週二執行時擷取到的教訓，會在週三自動套用，在代理啟動前自動注入。對大多數教訓——局部的、情境性的、廉價的——那就是它們正確的歸宿，迴圈到此為止。但當一條教訓被證明是永久性的，當它一次又一次持續適用，或者它揭露了代理的結構本身有問題時，自動注入就不再是答案了。

That’s when the two halves meet. I open Claude Code, point it at the agent and its learnings file, and have it merge the permanent lessons into the agent’s own instructions, then prune the entries it absorbed. My git log is full of commits like “bake in this run’s lessons for a clean next demo.” That particular commit exists because a 119KB Amazon page stalled a cheap model mid-run: the agent file now tells its builds to bound large pages, and I learned to validate any network agent with one real run, because health checks and mock runs miss page size, latency, and blocked sources every time. There’s even a commit correcting a learning that was itself wrong: the agent had blamed a port conflict for a failure that was actually Chrome auto-updating underneath it. Memory gets edited, not just appended. 這時候，兩半才真正接上。我打開 Claude Code，讓它看著那個代理和它的 learnings 檔案，把永久性的教訓併入代理自己的指令，然後把已吸收的條目刪掉。我的 git log 裡滿是這種 commit：「把這次執行的教訓烘進去，好讓下次示範乾淨一點。」那個 commit 之所以存在，是因為一個 119KB 的 Amazon 頁面在執行途中卡死了一個廉價模型：現在代理檔會告訴它的建構流程要對大型頁面設限；而我學到的是，任何會連網的代理都必須用一次「真實執行」來驗證，因為健康檢查和模擬執行每次都會漏掉頁面大小、延遲和被封鎖的來源。甚至還有一個 commit 是在修正「一條本身就錯了的教訓」：代理把一次失敗歸咎於連接埠衝突，實際上是 Chrome 在底下自動更新。記憶是會被修改的，不只是被追加。

So the full loop: a code agent writes the agent from the runtime’s own live manual. The agent runs, and writes down what it learns from failures and from my gate comments. Learnings override defaults immediately. And when a lesson proves structural, the code agent folds it into the file and the queue empties. Each pass, the file gets shorter and harder to fool. 所以完整的迴圈是：編程代理依據執行環境自己的即時手冊，寫出代理。代理執行，並把它從失敗和我的關卡留言中學到的東西寫下來。學到的教訓立刻覆蓋預設值。而當某條教訓被證明是結構性的，編程代理就把它折進檔案裡，佇列隨之清空。每繞一圈，這份檔案就更短一點，也更難被騙一點。

I’m building AgentUse, open-source infrastructure for autonomous agents, and the learning capture, the approval gates, and the self-serving manual above are all part of it. But the loop doesn’t depend on my stack. It depends on the three pieces existing at all: a live manual, a memory the runtime actually injects, and a scheduled appointment between your code agent and that memory. 我在做 AgentUse，一套給自主代理的開源基礎設施；上面講的教訓擷取、審批關卡、自我供應的手冊，全都是它的一部分。但這個迴圈並不依賴我的技術棧，它只依賴那三個元件的存在：一份即時的手冊、一份執行環境會真正注入的記憶，以及你的編程代理與那份記憶之間一個排定好的約會。

This week, take one agent, or one prompt you keep re-fixing, and give it a memory file the next run actually reads. Then put thirty minutes on the calendar to fold what accumulates back into the prompt. The first version of an agent is a guess. The file that’s been through this loop a dozen times is the only spec I trust. 這禮拜，挑一個代理，或一個你老是要重複修正的提示，給它一份「下次執行真的會讀」的記憶檔。然後在行事曆上排三十分鐘，把累積起來的東西折回提示裡。代理的第一版是一個猜測；跑過這個迴圈十幾遍的那份檔案，才是我唯一信任的規格書。

The Five Shapes of an Agent Fleet

代理艦隊的五種形態

Jul 16, 2026｜2026 年 7 月 16 日

A team of people used to run LifeHack. Today the company runs on agents, and I’m the only human left in the loop. LifeHack 過去是由一支團隊在運作的。今天，這家公司靠代理運轉，而我是迴圈裡僅存的人類。

The fleet is 129 agents at last count. They write, research, measure, render video, draft support replies, watch markets. Most of them came out of rebuilding LifeHack into an AI-first operation; the same patterns now run my consulting pipeline and my trading watchlists too. I never designed a taxonomy for any of it. But this week I audited the whole fleet, and every agent had settled into one of five shapes. Not roughly five. Five, with no stragglers: the ones that didn’t fit cleanly turned out to be misbuilt, and each was drifting toward one of the shapes anyway. 最近一次盤點，艦隊有 129 個代理。它們寫作、研究、量測、算圖出片、起草客服回覆、盯市場。大部分是在把 LifeHack 重建成 AI 優先組織的過程中長出來的；如今同一批模式也在跑我的顧問業務流程和交易觀察清單。我從來沒有替它們設計過任何分類法。但這週我盤點了整支艦隊，發現每一個代理都已經沉澱成五種形態中的一種。不是「大約五種」，是五種，一個掉隊的都沒有：那些套不乾淨的，後來都證明是蓋錯了，而且每一個本來就正在往其中某一種形態漂移。

That’s worth more than a tidy diagram. It means agent architecture isn’t a blank canvas where every pipeline is a fresh design decision. A few properties of the work generate the structure, the same way load and span generate a bridge. Once you can read those properties, the next agent designs itself. And if you’re rebuilding a human-run company into an agent-run one, as I did, the five shapes are the whole org chart. 這件事的價值遠大於一張漂亮的架構圖。它意味著：代理架構不是一張空白畫布、每條流程都得重新做一次設計決策。工作本身的幾個性質會自動生成結構，就像載重與跨距會生成一座橋。一旦你讀得懂那些性質，下一個代理就會自己設計自己。而如果你正像我一樣，把一家由人運作的公司重建成由代理運作的公司，這五種形態就是全部的組織圖。

The Three Questions｜三個問題

Three questions about the work decide which shape an agent takes. 關於工作本身的三個問題，決定了一個代理會長成哪種形態。

Does it change anything outside my system? An agent that only reads, measures, or writes internal files can be wrong cheaply. An agent that posts, sends, or schedules cannot. This question decides whether the structure needs an approval gate, a point where the loop stops and waits for a named person, and where that gate goes: directly in front of the action you can’t take back. 它會改變我系統之外的任何東西嗎？只讀取、量測、或寫內部檔案的代理，出錯的代價很低；會發布、寄送、或排程的代理則不然。這個問題決定了結構需不需要審批關卡——一個讓迴圈停下來、等待某個具名的人的點——以及那個關卡該擺在哪裡：就擺在那個你收不回來的動作正前方。

Does the draft keep overnight? A reply to a live conversation is stale in hours. A rendered video is exactly as good next Tuesday. Perishable work fuses creation and publishing into one run. Storable work lets you split them, and the split is where most of the interesting structure lives. 這份草稿放得過夜嗎？一則對進行中對話的回覆，幾小時就餿了；一支算好的影片，下週二一樣好用。會腐壞的工作，會把「創造」和「發布」熔進同一次執行；可儲存的工作則允許你把它們拆開——而大部分有趣的結構，就住在那個拆分處。

How many commitments are in the work? Posting a reply is one commitment. Shipping a blog post is two: putting a draft up for review under my name, then making it public. Each commitment is a separate decision, and separate decisions want separate stops. 這份工作裡包含幾次「承諾」？發一則回覆是一次承諾。出一篇部落格文章是兩次：先以我的名義把草稿送審，再把它公開。每一次承諾都是一個獨立的決定，而獨立的決定想要獨立的停靠點。

Ask those three questions of any job you’re handing to an agent, and one of five shapes falls out. 對任何你要交給代理的工作問這三個問題，五種形態中的一種就會自己掉出來。

Shape 1: The Worker｜形態一：工人

Reads, researches, measures, summarizes, writes internal state. No outside mutation, so no gate, no store, no manager. A scout that flags conversations worth joining. A measurement agent that pulls last week’s rankings. If one does its job badly, I delete a file. In a human org, this is the analyst: the research that’s on your desk each morning, no signature required. 讀取、研究、量測、摘要、寫入內部狀態。不改變外部世界，所以不需要關卡、不需要倉庫、不需要管理者。一個標記出「值得加入的對話」的偵察兵；一個拉取上週排名的量測代理。如果它做得爛，我刪掉一個檔案就好。在人類組織裡，這就是分析師：每天早上出現在你桌上的研究報告，不需要任何人簽名。

This is most of my fleet. Of my 129 agents, 106 never stop for a human, and the bulk of those are workers. That ratio is the point. Workers with no ceremony around them are what keep the ceremony meaningful everywhere else. If every agent stopped for me, approving would turn into a habit, and habit is where judgment goes to die. 這是我艦隊的大宗。129 個代理裡，有 106 個從不為人類停下，其中絕大多數是工人。這個比例正是重點：正因為工人身上沒有任何儀式，其他地方的儀式才保得住意義。如果每個代理都要為我停下來，批准就會淪為習慣，而習慣正是判斷力去送死的地方。

Shape 2: The Correspondent｜形態二：特派記者

One agent creates one perishable thing and publishes it in the same run. Drafting and posting can’t be separated, because the draft dies overnight. So the whole structure is a single file: find the target, write the draft, stop at the gate, post on approval. My reply agents and news-reaction agents are all this shape. 一個代理在同一次執行裡，創造一件會腐壞的東西並發布它。起草和發布無法分離，因為草稿放過夜就死了。所以整個結構就是單一檔案：找目標、寫草稿、在關卡停下、獲准後發布。我的回覆代理和新聞反應代理全都是這個形態。

In a human org, this is the correspondent: finds the story, files it, and it runs today with an editor’s sign-off, because by tomorrow it isn’t a story. The gate is inline, one line before the irreversible send. There’s nowhere else it could go. 在人類組織裡，這就是特派記者：找到故事、發回稿件，經編輯簽核後今天就見報，因為到了明天它就不是新聞了。關卡是內嵌的，就在那個不可逆的發送動作前一行。它不可能擺在別的地方。

Shape 3: The Dispatcher｜形態三：調度者

A manager that owns strategy and cadence, decides which agent runs today, and delegates. It looks senior, but notice what it never does: touch the outside world. Routing is reversible. Delegating is reversible. So the dispatcher itself carries no gate; every agent it dispatches keeps its own. 一個掌管策略與節奏的管理者，決定今天哪個代理該跑，然後把工作交派下去。它看起來位階很高，但注意它從不做的事：碰觸外部世界。路由是可逆的，交派是可逆的。所以調度者本身不帶關卡；它派出去的每個代理各自保有自己的關卡。

In a human org, this is the chief of staff: decides what gets done this week, and does none of it personally. The shape exists to hold the judgment that doesn’t belong in any single agent below it: how often to engage, which mix of activities, when to stand down. 在人類組織裡，這就是幕僚長：決定這週要完成什麼，但一件都不親自做。這個形態存在的目的，是收容那些不屬於底下任何單一代理的判斷：多久出手一次、活動的配比如何、什麼時候該按兵不動。

Shape 4: The Inventory Pipeline｜形態四：庫存型產線

When the artifact keeps, you can split creation from publishing, and the structure grows a warehouse in the middle. 當產出可以保存時，你就能把創造和發布拆開，而結構會在中間長出一座倉庫。

LifeHack’s short-video pipeline is the full version. Creator agents run on a schedule with no human anywhere: write the script, render the video with voiceover and captions, verify their own output (aspect ratio, duration, audio present), and file the result in a store. A manager runs separately and makes one decision per run: buffer below five, create more; stock ready and the calendar thin, publish one. Only the publish path stops for me, one gate at the exit, and what I approve there is whole: this video, this copy per platform, this slot across four channels. LifeHack 的短影音產線是完整版。創作代理照排程執行，全程沒有任何人類：寫腳本、算出帶旁白和字幕的影片、自行驗證輸出（畫面比例、長度、是否有音軌），然後把成品歸檔進倉庫。管理者獨立執行，每次只做一個決定：庫存低於五支就多做幾支；有現貨而行事曆很空，就發一支。只有發布路徑會為我停下——出口處一道關卡——而我在那裡批准的是一個完整的包裹：這支影片、每個平台的這段文案、四個頻道的這個時段。

The store is what earns the extra moving parts, in one specific place: rejection. When I reject a video at the gate with “the text is cropped on slide three,” the manager doesn’t shrug and end. It re-delegates to the creator in a re-create mode: same message, same audience, new render fixing the defect, and the new video comes back through the same gate. My rejection became an instruction instead of an ending. 倉庫之所以值得多出這些活動零件，關鍵只在一個地方：被退件的時候。當我在關卡上以「第三張投影片的文字被裁掉了」退回一支影片，管理者不會聳聳肩就結束。它會以「重製模式」重新交派給創作代理：同樣的訊息、同樣的受眾、重新算一版修好那個瑕疵，然後新影片再度回到同一道關卡。我的否決變成了一道指示，而不是一個句點。

One unglamorous component makes it safe: state. An item mid-approval is marked, so an overlapping scheduled run sees the mark and stands down instead of double-publishing. Boring, structural, and the difference between a pipeline and a race condition. 有一個毫不起眼的元件讓這一切安全：狀態。正在審批中的項目會被標記，所以一個時間重疊的排程執行看到標記就會退場，而不是重複發布。無聊、結構性，卻正是「產線」和「競態條件」之間的差別。

In a human org, this is the production studio: a small team builds ahead of schedule, and one editor at the exit decides what airs. 在人類組織裡，這就是製作工作室：一支小團隊超前進度地生產，出口處由一位編輯決定什麼上檔。

Shape 5: The Staged Pipeline｜形態五：分段式產線

Some work has more than one commitment in it, and each commitment ends a stage. LifeHack’s blog pipeline commits twice: a writer agent stops for approval on the draft, a release agent stops again before the post goes live. The LifeHack newsletter, the loop a team once produced by hand, runs the same way, and I’ve torn that one down in full before: pick the angle at the first stop, approve the rendered send at the second. 有些工作裡包含不只一次承諾，而每一次承諾就結束一個階段。LifeHack 的部落格產線承諾兩次：寫作代理在草稿處停下等待批准，發布代理在文章上線前再停一次。LifeHack 的電子報——那個曾經由一整支團隊手工產出的迴圈——也用同樣的方式運作，而我先前完整拆解過它：第一站挑角度，第二站批准那封算好的實際發信。

The shape looks like several agents chained together, but the chain isn’t decoration. Approving a draft and approving a release are different decisions made at different times with different information. Collapsing them into one stop means one of them is being made blind. An exec would recognize this instantly: it’s the two-signature process, and the second signature exists for the same reason it does on a wire transfer. 這個形態看起來像是好幾個代理串在一起，但那條鏈不是裝飾。批准草稿和批准發布，是在不同時間、根據不同資訊所做的不同決定。把它們壓縮成一站，等於其中一個決定是矇著眼做的。任何主管一眼就看得懂：這就是雙簽核流程，而第二個簽名存在的理由，跟電匯上第二個簽名存在的理由完全相同。

Reading Your Own Fleet｜讀懂你自己的艦隊

The taxonomy pays off in two directions. 這套分類法在兩個方向上都有回報。

Forward, it’s a design shortcut. Before building anything, ask the three questions. Nothing mutates: worker, and skip the ceremony. Mutates and perishable: correspondent, gate inline. Mutates and storable: inventory pipeline, gate at the exit. Multiple commitments: staged, one gate per stage. Strategy with no mutation of its own: dispatcher. The structure is decided before the first line is written. 往前看，它是設計的捷徑。在動手蓋任何東西之前，先問那三個問題。什麼都不改變：工人，省掉所有儀式。會改變外部且會腐壞：特派記者，關卡內嵌。會改變外部但可儲存：庫存型產線，關卡設在出口。多次承諾：分段式，每階段一道關卡。只有策略、自己不改變任何東西：調度者。結構在第一行還沒寫之前就已經決定了。

Backward, it’s a diagnostic. Every agent of mine that felt wrong in practice turned out to be wearing the wrong shape. A correspondent quietly doing strategy that belonged in a dispatcher. Approve the Decisions. Watch the Work.

批准決定，觀察工作

Jun 30, 2026｜2026 年 6 月 30 日

Everyone has worked for the manager who needs a status update every hour. Nothing ships without him seeing it first. He’s not malicious. He just can’t let go of the work itself. And the whole team slows to the speed of his attention, because that’s the actual bottleneck now. Him. 每個人都遇過那種每小時都要進度回報的主管。任何東西沒經他過目就不能出去。他並不壞，他只是放不下工作本身。於是整個團隊被拖慢到「他的注意力」的速度——因為真正的瓶頸現在就是他。

That manager is how most people supervise an AI agent. They wire an approval into every step, sign off again and again, and then wonder why the loop they built to save a day still eats one. They rebuilt the bottleneck. They just moved it from doing the work to approving it. 大多數人監督 AI 代理的方式，就是那個主管。他們在每一步都接上審批、一次又一次簽核，然後納悶為什麼這個「本來要省下一天」的迴圈還是吃掉一天。他們把瓶頸重建了一遍，只是從「做工作」搬到了「批准工作」。

The question I keep getting is when do you stop checking the agent. Wrong question. The real one is what you put your hand on while it runs. 我一直被問到的問題是：什麼時候可以不用再檢查代理？這是錯的問題。真正的問題是：在它跑的時候，你的手該按在哪裡。

There are two ways to get this wrong. Approve every step, and you’re the manager above, the bottleneck. Approve nothing, and one run does something you can’t take back. The fix isn’t the midpoint, gating every call the agent makes. Half of them it should just make on its own. You keep your hand on two: what you can’t undo, and the call that’s the actual point. Everything else, the work and the small reversible choices along the way, runs without you. You watch it. You don’t gate it. 搞砸這件事有兩種方式。每一步都批准，你就成了上面那個主管——瓶頸。什麼都不批准，某次執行就會做出你收不回來的事。解法不是取中間值、把代理做的每個判斷都設關卡；其中一半它本來就該自己決定。你的手只按在兩件事上：你收不回來的，以及那個真正是重點的判斷。其餘的一切——工作本身，以及沿途那些可逆的小選擇——都不需要你。你觀察它，但不攔它。

The calls you hold, the work you watch｜你握住的判斷，你觀察的工作

Two of the agent’s calls are yours. The one that’s the actual point: picking what’s worth doing. And the one you can’t take back. Those you gate, every time. The rest, the reversible calls it can make and you could undo, are the agent’s. You let them go. 代理的判斷裡有兩個是你的：那個真正是重點的——挑出什麼值得做；以及那個你收不回來的。這兩個每次都設關卡。其餘的，那些它做得了、你也還原得了的可逆判斷，是代理的。放手。

Here’s one I run with AgentUse. It turns my queued quote posts into images and ships them to X, Facebook, and Instagram, and it stops in exactly one place: before anything publishes. The agent checks what’s ready, builds the post, finds a slot, all on its own. Then it holds, because pushing something to a public account is the step it can’t take back. That gate is mine, and it sits exactly where the irreversible thing happens. 這是我用 AgentUse 跑的一個例子。它把我排隊中的金句貼文做成圖片，發到 X、Facebook 和 Instagram，而它只在一個地方停下：任何東西發布之前。檢查什麼已就緒、組出貼文、找到時段，全都自己來。然後它停住，因為把東西推上一個公開帳號，是那個它收不回來的步驟。那道關卡是我的，而且它就坐落在不可逆的事情發生的位置上。

A loop can have more than one of these, and they don’t all sit at the end. My newsletter stops twice: once in the middle, the moment it’s laid out the week’s candidate angles, for me to pick the one worth saying, and again at the end for me to approve the send. The first one is mid-run, the agent’s already done a chunk of work before it stops, and that’s fine. A mid-run gate isn’t the problem. The gate belongs wherever one of these calls lives, and a call can land anywhere in the run. One agent split by three approvals is clean if each one is a call that’s actually yours. 一個迴圈可以有不只一道這種關卡，而且它們不見得都在結尾。我的電子報停兩次：一次在中間，在它把本週候選角度攤開的那一刻，讓我挑出真正值得說的那個；一次在最後，讓我批准發送。第一次是執行途中，代理在停下來之前已經做了一大塊工作——這完全沒問題。「途中停」本身不是問題。關卡該待在這兩種判斷所在之處，而判斷可能落在執行過程的任何位置。一個代理被三道審批切開也很乾淨，只要每一道都真的是屬於你的判斷。

The test is never where the gate falls in time. It’s this: is this a call only I should make, the irreversible or the essential, or am I babysitting work the agent should just do? 判準從來不是關卡落在時間軸的哪裡，而是這個：這是不是一個只有我該做的判斷——不可逆的、或本質性的——還是我在保姆式地盯著一件代理本來就該自己做的工作？

The execution isn’t｜執行不屬於你

The execution is everything between the decisions. The drafting, the formatting, the assembly, the calls to tools. None of it needs your judgment, and every approval you staple onto it is just you in the way. 執行，就是決定與決定之間的一切：起草、排版、組裝、呼叫工具。這些都不需要你的判斷；你釘在上面的每一道審批，都只是你自己擋在路中間。

This is the manager from the top of the piece. An employee who stops every twenty minutes to ask “is this okay so far?” isn’t being managed. He’s being blocked. And he never builds the judgment to run without you, because you never let a single stretch of work finish without your hand on it. Gate the execution and you cap the loop’s throughput at your availability, which was the one thing you were trying to escape. You also tell the agent you don’t trust it to execute, only to start and stop, which is another way of saying you don’t trust it at all. 這就是文章開頭那個主管。一個每二十分鐘就停下來問「目前這樣可以嗎」的員工，不叫被管理，叫被卡住。而且他永遠養不出獨立運作的判斷力，因為你從沒讓任何一段工作在你不插手的情況下完成過。替執行設關卡，等於把整個迴圈的吞吐量上限鎖死在「你有沒有空」上——而那正是你原本想逃離的東西。你同時也在告訴代理：我不信任你執行，只信任你啟動和停止——換句話說，就是完全不信任你。

So when you feel the urge to add a gate, ask what it’s sitting on. One of your two calls, the irreversible or the essential, keep it. Anything else, a step or a choice the agent can undo, and you’re rebuilding the bottleneck. 所以當你有衝動想加一道關卡時，先問它坐在什麼東西上面。如果是你那兩種判斷之一——不可逆的、或本質性的——留著。若是別的，任何代理自己還原得了的步驟或選擇，那你就是在重建瓶頸。

Watch the work, don’t block it｜觀察工作，別擋住它

Keeping your hand off the execution does not mean going blind. This is the other ditch people drive into. They think the only two options are approve every step or ignore everything until the end. 把手從執行上拿開，不等於閉上眼睛。這是人們會掉進去的另一條溝：他們以為只有兩個選項——每一步都批准，或者從頭到尾不看直到最後。

There’s a third, and it’s the whole game: watch without stopping. Observability. I replay exactly what the agent did and why, step by step, after the run, without ever blocking the run. I’m reading its real reasoning, not its final summary. 還有第三個，而它才是整場遊戲的關鍵：不阻擋地觀察。可觀測性。我在執行結束後，逐步重播代理究竟做了什麼、為什麼那樣做，全程從不阻擋執行。我讀的是它真實的推理過程，不是它的最終摘要。

That’s what a good manager actually does. He doesn’t approve every paragraph his writer drafts. He reads the work, keeps a feel for how it’s going, and steps in at the decisions. Observability is that, for agents. It’s how you supervise the work without standing in it. 一個好主管實際上就是這樣做的。他不會批准他的寫手起草的每一個段落；他讀那些作品、對進展保持感覺，然後在決定的節點上介入。可觀測性就是代理版的這件事——它讓你監督工作，而不站在工作中間。

And it changes what the review even is. 而它也改變了「審查」這件事本身的性質。

The review is a decision, on evidence｜審查是一個決定，而且要有證據

Approving the output is itself a decision, the last one, so it gets a gate. But the gate isn’t a thumbs-up on a final answer floating free of context. It’s the output plus the trace of how the agent got there. I approve the rendered newsletter, the real email as it will land, and I can see the run behind it. Evidence, not vibes. 批准產出本身就是一個決定——最後一個——所以它有一道關卡。但這道關卡不是對一個脫離脈絡、憑空飄著的最終答案比讚。它是「產出」加上「代理如何走到這裡的軌跡」。我批准的是算好的電子報，是它落到收件匣裡的真實模樣，而且我看得到背後那次執行。憑證據，不憑感覺。

Whether that gate ever comes off depends on one thing, and it isn’t how long the agent has behaved. It’s what a mistake costs and whether you can take it back. 這道關卡有沒有一天會拿掉，只取決於一件事，而那件事不是代理已經乖了多久，而是：一個錯誤的代價是什麼，以及你收不收得回來。

Reversible and cheap: once the replay has shown me enough clean runs, I drop the gate and let it ship on its own, and I keep watching the trace. Irreversible and expensive: I keep my hand on it for as long as the company exists. The newsletter send goes to the whole list and can’t be unsent, so I press send myself, forever, and it has nothing to do with the agent being unreliable. It’s the most reliable thing I run. The support agent drafts replies and never sends the refunds and cancellations, because a wrong one is a real person and real money I can’t claw back. Drafting is reversible. Sending is not. That line, not the agent’s track record, decides which decisions stay gated. 可逆而廉價的：一旦重播讓我看夠了乾淨的執行，我就把關卡拿掉、讓它自己出貨，同時繼續盯著軌跡。不可逆而昂貴的：只要公司還在，我的手就一直按在上面。電子報一發就是整份名單，收不回來，所以那個發送鍵我自己按，永遠如此——而這跟代理可不可靠毫無關係，它其實是我跑過最可靠的東西。客服代理會起草回覆，但永遠不會自己送出退款和取消，因為一次弄錯，另一端是一個真實的人和一筆我要不回來的錢。起草是可逆的，送出不是。決定哪些判斷要繼續設關卡的，是這條界線，不是代理的過往紀錄。

The arc is just onboarding｜這條曲線，就是新人上手的過程

Put it together and it’s the shape of training anyone. 把這些拼起來，就是訓練任何一個人的形狀。

Week one, you brief the new hire, you read everything they produced, you approve before it goes out. A month in, you brief them, you skim, you only sign off on the things that can’t be undone. A year in, you brief them and the only thing that reaches you is the exception they flagged themselves, because they learned to raise a hand on the weird cases instead of guessing. 第一週，你交代新人任務，讀他產出的每一樣東西，在發出去前逐件批准。一個月後，你交代任務，快速掃過，只在不可逆的事情上簽核。一年後，你交代任務，而唯一會到你面前的，是他自己標記出來的例外——因為他學會了在奇怪的案例上舉手，而不是自己亂猜。

The agent is the same arc. What changes isn’t the agent. It’s you moving from reading every trace to trusting that the trace exists and watching the number. An agent that never escalates isn’t reliable, by the way. It’s just quiet about being wrong. The hand-raise is the thing you’re really training. 代理走的是同一條曲線。改變的不是代理，而是你——從「讀每一份軌跡」轉變為「信任軌跡存在、盯著數字看」。順帶一提，一個從不上報的代理不叫可靠，它只是把自己的錯誤悶著不說。你真正在訓練的，是那個舉手的動作。

And it runs backward too. The week the replay starts showing sloppy steps, or one bad output slips the last gate, I go back to reading every run and the gate moves back up. Trust is revocable because the evidence is revocable. Nothing about last month protects you when the model under it shifted or the inputs drifted and the instructions didn’t. 而且這條曲線是雙向的。哪一週重播開始出現草率的步驟，或者一份爛產出溜過了最後一道關卡，我就回去讀每一次執行，關卡也往回升。信任是可以撤回的，因為證據是可以撤回的。當底層模型換了、或輸入漂移而指令沒跟上時，上個月的表現保護不了你分毫。

So stop asking how much you trust the agent. Ask what you’re holding. The two calls that are yours, on the evidence of how it got there. And nothing else. Watch the work the whole time, and never once stand in it. 所以別再問你有多信任這個代理了。問你手上握著什麼。那兩個屬於你的判斷，依據它如何走到這裡的證據來做。其餘一概不握。全程觀察工作，但一次都別站在裡面。

The judgment was always the job. The work was never yours to hold. 判斷一直都是你的工作。工作本身，從來就不是你該握著的東西。

You Don’t Trust the Agent. You Trust the Loop.

你信任的不是代理，是那個迴圈

Jun 18, 2026｜2026 年 6 月 18 日

A friend asked me this week how I get an agent reliable enough to trust what it produces. He builds too, so this was not idle curiosity. He had a few agents running well, but he wasn’t ready to let any of them produce work he hadn’t checked. Which is the right instinct. 這禮拜有個朋友問我，要怎麼把一個代理做到可靠得足以信任它的產出。他自己也在做開發，所以這不是閒聊式的好奇。他手上有幾個跑得不錯的代理，但還沒準備好讓任何一個產出他沒檢查過的東西。這是正確的直覺。

He was working from the assumption almost everyone starts with, including me. Reliability is something you verify up front. You read the output, you decide it is good, and then you trust it. Like checking a spec sheet before you buy the part. 他抱著幾乎每個人一開始都會有的假設，包括我在內：可靠性是一件你「事先驗證」的事。你讀產出、判定它夠好，然後開始信任它——就像買零件前先看規格表。

My answer was shorter than he expected. You don’t. Not on day one. 我的回答比他預期的短：你不會信任它。第一天不會。

Day one you should trust an agent about as much as you trust a new hire on their first morning. You would not hand a new hire the company card because the resume was strong. You watch them work. You correct them. A month later you trust their judgment, and the thing that changed was not the person. It was that you did the watching. 第一天，你對一個代理的信任程度，應該跟你對一個第一天早上報到的新人差不多。你不會因為履歷漂亮就把公司信用卡交給新人。你看著他工作、糾正他；一個月後你信任他的判斷了，而改變的不是那個人，是你做了「觀看」這件事。

Trust is not a property the agent has. It is the output of a loop you run around it. 信任不是代理身上的一種屬性，而是你圍繞它跑的那個迴圈所產出的東西。

The loop is simple: run it, watch what it actually did, correct it, then feed that correction back into what it reads before the next run. 迴圈很簡單：跑它、看它實際做了什麼、糾正它，然後把糾正餵回它下次執行前會讀到的內容裡。

There are three stages. You do the first once, to set the target. The second and third are the loop. Let me show you with the one agent I would actually let near my customers. 一共三個階段。第一個你只做一次，用來設定標的；第二和第三個才是迴圈。我用一個「我真的敢放到客戶身邊」的代理來示範。

Stage 1: write the first version｜階段一：寫出第一版

The first version is where you state what you want before the agent runs once. The objective. What good output looks like. The judgment calls you want locked in so the agent does not invent its own. Then you give it tools. 第一版，是你在代理跑第一次之前，把你要什麼講清楚：目標是什麼、好的產出長什麼樣、哪些判斷你要鎖死好讓代理不會自己發明一套。然後你給它工具。

I write the agent itself as an AgentUse agent, a single file that holds its instructions, its schedule, and its tools. The authoring happens in Claude Code with the AgentUse skill, so I am describing the agent in plain language and the skill turns that into the actual config. 我把代理本身寫成一個 AgentUse 代理——一個檔案，裝著它的指令、排程和工具。撰寫過程在 Claude Code 裡搭配 AgentUse skill 完成，所以我只是用日常語言描述這個代理，由 skill 把它轉成實際的設定檔。

My customer support agent is a clean example. The objective is small on purpose: work a bounded batch of the support inbox, never the whole thing. Good output is a drafted reply, classified correctly. The judgment is written down in plain rules. Only the support mailbox. Three conversations per run, no more. Cold outreach gets archived. Anything about billing, a refund, a cancellation, or a complaint gets a draft, an internal note, and a tag that says a human needs to look. For tools I hand it a skill, the helpscout-cs-agent skill, which already knows how to read a conversation, check the customer’s status, and apply an action. Most of what I give an agent now is skills. Sometimes I write a new one for the job. 我的客服代理是個乾淨的例子。目標刻意設得很小：處理客服信箱裡「有界限的一批」，絕不是全部。好的產出，是一份分類正確的回覆草稿。判斷則被寫成樸素的規則：只碰客服信箱；每次執行最多三則對話，不得更多；陌生開發信直接封存；任何牽涉帳務、退款、取消或客訴的，一律產出草稿、內部備註，以及一個「需要人類看一下」的標籤。工具方面我給它一個 skill——helpscout-cs-agent——它已經知道怎麼讀一則對話、查客戶狀態、執行一個動作。現在我給代理的東西，大部分都是 skills；偶爾為了某項工作再寫一個新的。

Version one is a hypothesis, not a product. I wrote down what good looked like and let it run. I did not expect it to be right. 第一版是一個假設，不是一個成品。我寫下「好」長什麼樣，然後讓它跑。我從沒指望它會是對的。

Stage 2: the supervised run｜階段二：受監督的執行

Then you watch. Not the summary it gives you at the end. The actual run. 接著你觀看。不是它最後給你的那份摘要，是實際的執行過程。

I use two things in AgentUse for this. The session viewer, which replays exactly what the agent did and why, step by step, so I am reading its real reasoning instead of its final answer. And the approval gate, which stops the agent on any mutation and waits for my sign-off before anything touches the real world. 我在 AgentUse 裡用兩樣東西做這件事。一是 session viewer，逐步重播代理究竟做了什麼、為什麼那樣做，讓我讀到的是它真實的推理，而不是它的最終答案。二是審批關卡，在任何會造成變更的動作前把代理攔下來，等我簽核之後才讓東西碰到真實世界。

There are two ways to gate, and the support agent uses the stricter one. The most important line in it is this: never send replies, draft only. The agent is not allowed to do the one thing it cannot take back. A wrong refund answer to a real customer is real damage. Money, trust, and a person on the other end who now thinks we do not know our own policy. So the agent drafts. I read. It sends nothing on its own. For agents where I do want the action to happen but only with my eyes on it, I use the AgentUse approval gate instead, so the mutation runs the moment I approve and not before. 設關卡有兩種方式，客服代理用的是比較嚴的那種。它裡面最重要的一行是：永不發送回覆，只起草。這個代理根本不被允許去做那件收不回來的事。一個錯誤的退款回覆送到真實客戶手上，是真實的傷害——金錢、信任，以及另一端一個現在認為「這家公司連自己的政策都搞不清楚」的人。所以代理起草，我閱讀，它自己什麼都不送。至於那些「我確實希望動作發生、但要在我眼皮底下發生」的代理，我改用 AgentUse 的審批關卡，讓變更在我批准的那一刻執行，而不是更早。

This is the part people skip. They wire an agent straight to the irreversible action, then act surprised when it does something irreversible. Gate the mutation. Let the agent propose. Keep the commit for yourself, at least until you have watched it enough. 這是大家會跳過的部分。他們把代理直接接到不可逆的動作上，然後在它做出不可逆的事情時裝作很驚訝。替變更設關卡。讓代理去「提案」。把「拍板」留給自己——至少在你看夠之前。

Stage 3: tune the instructions｜階段三：調校指令

Based on what you saw, you go back and sharpen. Back to Claude Code and the AgentUse skill, editing the agent’s instructions to close the gaps you just watched it fall into. 根據你看到的東西，回頭把它磨利。回到 Claude Code 和 AgentUse skill，修改代理的指令，把你剛剛親眼看它掉進去的那些縫隙補起來。

Here is the part I like. I built a second AgentUse agent whose only job is to learn. It runs on a schedule, every Sunday morning, reads the last seven days of replies that actually went out, the ones I edited before they sent, finds the patterns in how I changed them, and rewrites the knowledge base the drafting agent reads. My corrections do not live in my head. They become the instructions the next draft starts from. 這是我喜歡的部分。我另外建了一個 AgentUse 代理，它唯一的工作就是學習。它照排程執行，每週日早上讀過去七天真正寄出去的回覆——也就是我在發送前編輯過的那些——找出我修改它們的模式，然後重寫起草代理所讀的知識庫。我的糾正不再只存在我腦袋裡，它們變成了下一份草稿的起點。

That is what closes the loop. The agent drafts. I fix. A second agent turns my fixes into next week’s starting point. Week over week the drafts need less editing, because the thing the agent reads before it drafts is shaped by every correction I have already made. 這才是把迴圈閉合的關鍵。代理起草，我修正，第二個代理把我的修正變成下週的起點。一週又一週，草稿需要的編輯越來越少，因為代理下筆前所讀的東西，已經被我過去做過的每一次修正給塑造過了。

The next move tightens it further. AgentUse is adding learning to the approval gate itself, so instead of a weekly agent harvesting my corrections, the comment I leave when I review a draft teaches the agent the reason on the spot. Not “change this word” but “you keep being too stiff on cancellations, warm it up.” The edit becomes a lesson the moment I make it, which folds stage 2 and stage 3 into one motion. 下一步會把它收得更緊。AgentUse 正在把「學習」直接加進審批關卡本身，這樣就不必靠一個每週執行的代理來收割我的修正——我審稿時留下的那句留言，會當場教會代理背後的理由。不是「改掉這個字」，而是「你在處理取消時老是太生硬，把語氣放暖一點」。修改在我做出的那一刻就變成了教訓，這等於把階段二和階段三折成同一個動作。

What the loop actually buys you｜這個迴圈到底替你換到什麼

That is the whole method. Three stages: you do the first once to set the target, and the last two are the loop you run as many times as it takes. Though here is what surprises people: a single pass through the loop often produces something good enough to use. You are not trapped waiting for a broken thing to work. You are sharpening one that mostly works already, and most of the sharpening happens in the first few passes. 整套方法就是這樣。三個階段：第一個做一次以設定標的，後兩個是你要跑幾次就跑幾次的迴圈。不過有件事會讓人意外：只繞一圈，往往就能產出「好到可以用」的東西。你不是被困在原地等一個壞掉的東西修好，你是在打磨一個本來就大致能用的東西——而且大部分的打磨都發生在前幾圈。

So when my friend asks how I trust the output, the honest answer is that I don’t trust the output. I trust the loop that produces it. The agent on day one is a guess. The agent after ten passes is something I watched get better, with my own corrections baked into what it reads before it writes a word. 所以當我朋友問我怎麼信任那些產出時，誠實的答案是：我不信任產出，我信任產出它的那個迴圈。第一天的代理是一個猜測；跑過十圈之後的代理，是我親眼看著變好的東西，而且我自己的糾正早已被烘進它下筆前所讀的內容裡。

Trust was never something I granted the agent. It is the residue of the loop. 信任從來不是我「授予」代理的東西，它是那個迴圈留下的沉澱物。

Build the loop first. The trust shows up on its own. 先把迴圈建起來，信任會自己出現。

You Don’t Need Fewer Agents. You Need Agents That Don’t Need You.

你需要的不是更少的代理，而是不需要你的代理

May 29, 2026｜2026 年 5 月 29 日

A few weeks ago I caught myself with fourteen terminal tabs open, each one an AI agent doing something. One was refactoring a script. One was drafting a newsletter. One was digging through support tickets. Two were stuck waiting on me and I had forgotten which two. 幾週前，我發現自己開著十四個終端機分頁，每一個裡面都有一個 AI 代理在做事。一個在重構腳本，一個在起草電子報，一個在翻客服工單。有兩個卡住了在等我，而我已經忘記是哪兩個。

It felt like a whole team was working for me. I was also exhausted by noon. Both of those were true at the same time, and it took me a while to admit they had the same cause. 感覺就像有一整支團隊在替我工作。同時，我中午就累垮了。這兩件事同時為真，而我花了好一陣子才承認它們的成因是同一個。

I was the thing all fourteen agents were waiting for. And there is one of me. 那十四個代理都在等的東西，就是我。而我只有一個。

Addy Osmani gave this feeling a name｜Addy Osmani 替這種感覺取了名字

Addy Osmani wrote a piece this week called The Orchestration Tax, and it put a clean edge on something I had been feeling without language for it. The argument is simple. Starting an agent is cheap. It is a sentence. Closing the loop on that agent is expensive, because someone has to read what came back, decide if it is right, and reconcile it with everything the other agents touched. That someone is you. There is one of you. Addy Osmani 這禮拜寫了一篇叫〈The Orchestration Tax（協調稅）〉的文章，把我一直感覺得到卻說不出口的東西，切出了一道乾淨的邊界。論點很簡單：啟動一個代理很便宜，一句話的事；把那個代理的迴圈閉合起來卻很貴，因為得有人去讀回傳的東西、判斷它對不對、再把它跟其他代理動過的所有東西整合起來。那個人就是你。而你只有一個。

He borrows the best metaphor for it from programming. Python has a Global Interpreter Lock, the GIL. You can spawn a hundred threads, but only one runs real work at a time, because they all have to grab the same single lock. You are the GIL of your agents. They can all run. But the moment any of their work needs judgment, that work has to wait for the one lock. You hold it. You are the slow serial part of a fast parallel system. 他從程式設計借來了最貼切的比喻。Python 有個全域直譯器鎖，也就是 GIL。你可以生出一百條執行緒，但同一時間只有一條在做真正的工作，因為它們全都得搶同一把鎖。你就是你那些代理的 GIL。它們全都能跑，但只要它們的工作需要判斷，那份工作就得排隊等那把唯一的鎖——而鎖在你手上。你是一個高速平行系統裡那個緩慢的序列部分。

He got there on a Google I/O panel with Richard Seroter, Aja Hammerly, and Ciera Jaspan. Richard is the one who named it. “You can’t manage twenty agents successfully in your own brain.” He is right. Feeling busy and being productive came apart for me the same way they did for Addy. 他是在一場 Google I/O 座談上想通這件事的，同場的還有 Richard Seroter、Aja Hammerly 和 Ciera Jaspan。是 Richard 講出了那句話：「你沒辦法光靠自己的腦袋成功管理二十個代理。」他說得對。「感覺很忙」和「真的有產出」，在我身上就像在 Addy 身上一樣，脫鉤了。

Read his piece. It is the best diagnosis of this problem I have seen. 去讀他那篇。那是我看過對這個問題最好的診斷。

But I think most people are about to draw the wrong conclusion from it. 但我認為大多數人正要從中得出錯誤的結論。

The wrong lesson is “run fewer agents”｜錯誤的結論是「少跑幾個代理」

The obvious takeaway is to throttle yourself. Scale your fleet down to the number of agents you can personally review, which for most of us is two or three. Slow the producer to match the consumer. Backpressure. 最直觀的結論是節流自己：把艦隊縮到你本人審查得完的數量，對大多數人來說是兩三個。把生產端放慢，去配合消費端。也就是反壓。

That is good advice for the agents you have today. It is the wrong thing to optimize. 對你今天手上的那些代理來說，這是好建議。但它不是該被最佳化的東西。

Here is the part the GIL metaphor hides. The tax is not paid per agent. It is paid per open thread to your attention. An agent you babysit in a chat window holds a thread open the entire time it works. Every turn, it stops and looks at you. Twenty of those is unmanageable, and Addy is completely right that it is. But the count was never the real problem. The architecture of each agent was. 這是 GIL 這個比喻藏起來的部分：這筆稅不是按「每個代理」課的，而是按「每一條掛在你注意力上的開啟中執行緒」課的。一個你在聊天視窗裡當保姆盯著的代理，在它工作的整段期間都掛著一條執行緒；每一輪它都停下來看著你。二十個這種東西當然管不了，Addy 說得完全正確。但數量從來不是真正的問題，每個代理的架構才是。

Most of what people call “running agents” is sitting in a terminal tab, watching an agent think, approving each step, nudging it when it drifts. That is not delegation. That is pair programming with something that types faster than you. Of course it does not scale. You did not remove yourself from the loop. You just moved your chair closer to it. 大多數人口中的「跑代理」，其實是坐在一個終端機分頁前，看著代理思考、逐步批准、在它跑偏時推它一把。那不叫委派，那叫跟一個打字比你快的東西結對程式設計。它當然無法規模化——你並沒有把自己移出迴圈，你只是把椅子往迴圈搬得更近。

The fix is not fewer of those. The fix is to stop building those. 解法不是少做幾個那樣的東西，而是不要再做那樣的東西。

Two species of agent｜兩種截然不同的代理物種

There are two completely different things wearing the word “agent” right now, and we keep talking about them as if they were one. 現在有兩種完全不同的東西共用「代理」這個詞，而我們一直把它們當成同一種東西在討論。

The first is the assistant in a terminal tab. You prompt, it responds, you steer, it responds again. It is brilliant for hard, fuzzy work where the judgment is the whole point. It is also a continuous claim on your attention. You cannot walk away. The loop only closes when you close it, by hand, every time. 第一種是終端機分頁裡的助理。你下提示、它回應、你導引、它再回應。對於困難而模糊、判斷本身就是重點的工作，它非常出色。但它同時是對你注意力的持續索求——你不能走開。那個迴圈只有在你親手閉合它時才會閉合，每一次都要。

The second is the autonomous agent. It is not a conversation. It is a thing you wrote down once. It runs on its own, does its job, checks its own work, and only comes back to you at one gate you decided on in advance. When it is not at the gate, it holds zero threads to your attention. You can have ten of them running and feel nothing, because none of them is sitting in a terminal tab waiting for you to look. 第二種是自主代理。它不是一段對話，而是一件你寫下來一次的東西。它自己跑、做它的工作、檢查自己的成果，只在一個你事先決定好的關卡回來找你。當它不在關卡上時，它掛在你注意力上的執行緒是零。你可以同時跑十個而毫無感覺，因為沒有任何一個坐在終端機分頁裡等你看它。

Addy himself pointed at this. In his five fixes, four are about rationing your attention better. Sort the work. Batch the reviews. Protect your serial time. Give agents a long leash. All good. All discipline. The fifth one is different in kind: only spend the lock on judgment, and make the agent prove the boring eighty percent itself with a passing test or a screenshot. Addy 自己也指向了這一點。他提的五個解法裡，有四個是關於如何更好地配給你的注意力：把工作排序、把審查批次化、保護你的序列時間、給代理長一點的韁繩。都很好，都是紀律。第五個則是不同性質的：只把那把鎖花在判斷上，並且要求代理用一個通過的測試或一張截圖，自己證明那無聊的八成。

That fifth one is the only structural fix in the list. The other four help you survive the tax. The fifth removes the work from your lock entirely. So I would bet everything on the fifth and treat the rest as coping. 第五個是清單裡唯一的結構性解法。其他四個幫你「撐過」這筆稅，第五個則直接把工作從你的鎖裡移走。所以我會把全部賭注押在第五個上，其餘的都只當成應付手段。

The autonomous agent is what the fifth fix looks like when you build a whole thing around it. 自主代理，就是當你圍繞著第五個解法蓋出一整套東西時，它長成的樣子。

What that actually looks like｜實際上長什麼樣

I write mine with AgentUse. An agent there is not a chat session. It is a markdown file. You describe the job in plain English, list the tools it can touch, tell it how to verify its own work, and set one approval gate for the single decision that needs you. Then you give it a schedule, and the thing runs itself. 我的代理是用 AgentUse 寫的。在那裡，一個代理不是一段聊天工作階段，而是一個 markdown 檔案。你用日常語言描述工作、列出它能碰的工具、告訴它如何驗證自己的成果，並為那個唯一需要你的決定設一道審批關卡。然後給它一份排程，這東西就自己跑了。

One I use every week reads our newsletter performance, the recent subscriber replies, and the support themes from the last seven days. It drafts a brief. It checks the brief against the last few sends so it does not repeat an angle we already used. Then it stops at one gate and shows me the draft and the reasoning. I spend ninety seconds on the only part that needs me, which is the judgment call. The hour of reading, cross-referencing, and reconciling that used to be mine never enters my lock at all. 我每週都會用的其中一個，會讀我們電子報的成效數據、近期訂閱者的回覆，以及過去七天客服的主題。它草擬一份簡報，並拿這份簡報跟前幾次發送比對，避免重複已經用過的切角。然後它停在一道關卡，把草稿和推理過程攤給我看。我只花九十秒在唯一需要我的部分，也就是那個判斷。過去屬於我的那一小時閱讀、交叉比對和整合，從頭到尾都沒有進入我的鎖。

Compare that to the terminal-tab version of the same task. I would open a session, paste the data, watch it work, correct it twice, ask it to check itself, and close the loop manually. Same output. But one of them taxes me for an hour and one of them taxes me for ninety seconds, because one holds a thread open the whole time and the other holds none until the gate. 拿它跟同一件事的「終端機分頁版」比較：我會開一個工作階段、貼上資料、看著它做、糾正兩次、要它自我檢查，然後手動閉合迴圈。產出一樣。但一個向我課一小時的稅，另一個只課九十秒——因為一個全程掛著一條執行緒，另一個在抵達關卡之前一條都沒有。

Think of a space heater with no thermostat. You are the feedback loop. You keep getting up, feeling the room, twisting the dial, sitting back down, getting up again. A thermostat is the same heater with the loop closed inside it. You set the target once. It only interrupts you when something happens that it cannot handle on its own. Most people are running their AI like a space heater and calling themselves busy because they never stop walking to the dial. 想像一台沒有恆溫器的電暖器。你就是那個回饋迴圈：你不斷站起來、感受室溫、轉一下旋鈕、坐下、再站起來。恆溫器就是同一台電暖器，只是迴圈被閉合在它內部。你設定一次目標，只有當發生它處理不了的狀況時才會打擾你。大多數人跑 AI 的方式就像那台沒有恆溫器的電暖器，然後因為自己一直在走去轉旋鈕，就宣稱自己很忙。

AgentUse, or whatever you build this with, is how you put the thermostat in. AgentUse，或任何你用來蓋這東西的工具，就是你把恆溫器裝進去的方式。

Why this beats Amdahl instead of obeying it｜為什麼這是打敗 Amdahl 定律，而不是順從它

Amdahl’s Law says your speedup is capped by the part of the work that stays serial. Addy is right that in agent work, the serial part is your judgment, and spawning more agents does not speed up your judgment. It just deepens the queue feeding into it. Amdahl 定律說：你的加速幅度受限於那些必須序列執行的部分。Addy 說得對，在代理工作裡，序列的部分就是你的判斷；生出更多代理並不會加速你的判斷，只會讓餵進去的佇列更深。

But you do not beat Amdahl by adding cores. You beat it by shrinking the serial fraction. That is the whole game. 但你不是靠加核心來打敗 Amdahl 的，你是靠縮小序列比例。整場遊戲就在這裡。

A terminal-tab agent has a fat serial fraction, because reading raw output, deciding if it is right, and reconciling it are all yours. An autonomous agent that writes its own passing test and hands you a verified result has a thin one, because the verification moved out of your lock and into the machine. You are no longer reviewing whether the work is correct. You are reviewing whether the correct work is the right work. That is a smaller, sharper question, and it is the only one worth your attention anyway. 終端機分頁裡的代理，序列比例很肥，因為讀原始輸出、判斷對不對、整合，全都是你的事。一個會自己寫出通過的測試、然後遞給你一個已驗證結果的自主代理，序列比例很瘦，因為驗證這件事已經從你的鎖裡搬進了機器。你不再是在審查「這份工作正不正確」，而是在審查「這份正確的工作，是不是對的工作」。那是一個更小、更銳利的問題，而且它本來就是唯一值得你注意力的問題。

So the backpressure ceiling Addy describes is real, but it is not fixed. Raise your effective review rate by making most of the work arrive pre-verified, and the ceiling moves up. You can run more agents, not fewer, because each one now asks less of you. The constraint was never the count. It was how much of your lock each agent demanded. 所以 Addy 描述的那個反壓天花板確實存在，但它不是固定的。讓大部分工作抵達時已經預先驗證過，你的有效審查速率就提高，天花板也跟著往上移。你可以跑更多代理，而不是更少，因為每一個現在向你要的東西變少了。限制從來不是數量，而是每個代理索求了你多少鎖的時間。

One thing to do this week｜這禮拜做一件事

Find the one agent you babysit the most. The chat session you keep reopening for the same recurring task. The one that taxes you every single time because you are its loop. 找出你最常當保姆盯著的那一個代理——那個你為了同一件重複性工作一再重開的聊天工作階段，那個每次都向你課稅的，因為你就是它的迴圈。

Write it down as a file instead. Describe the job, give it the tools, tell it how to check its own work, and set one gate for the part that needs you. Run it once by hand to see if it holds. Then put it on a schedule and walk away. 改成把它寫成一個檔案。描述工作、給它工具、告訴它如何檢查自己的成果，並替那個需要你的環節設一道關卡。手動跑一次看它撐不撐得住，然後給它一份排程，走開。

You will not have one fewer agent. You will have one fewer thread pulling on the one resource you cannot clone. 你不會因此少一個代理，你會少一條拉扯著「那個你複製不了的資源」的執行緒。

That is the whole move. Spawning agents is not the skill. Anyone can open twenty tabs. The skill is building the ones that do not need you to be standing there, so the only thing you spend your attention on is the thing only you can do. 整套動作就是這樣。生出代理不是本事，誰都能開二十個分頁。本事是做出那些「不需要你站在旁邊」的代理，好讓你的注意力只花在那件只有你做得到的事情上。

Open Loop Companies Forget. Closed Loop Companies Compound.

開迴圈的公司會遺忘，閉迴圈的公司會複利

Apr 28, 2026｜2026 年 4 月 28 日

When I ran LifeHack with a team, I sat through a leadership review where someone asked, “what did we actually learn last quarter?” The room went quiet. Not because nobody had worked. Everyone had worked hard. The team had shipped, sold, hired, and put out fires. The silence was because nobody could reconstruct what had actually happened. 當我還帶著團隊經營 LifeHack 時，我坐在一場管理層檢討會上，有人問：「上一季我們到底學到了什麼？」全場安靜。不是因為沒人做事——每個人都很拚，團隊出了貨、賣了東西、招了人、滅了火。那份沉默，是因為沒有人能重建出「究竟發生過什麼」。

That silence is not a culture problem. It is not a discipline problem. It is a system problem. 那份沉默不是文化問題，也不是紀律問題，而是系統問題。

The company was an open loop. It executed, but it did not learn from itself. 那家公司是一個開迴圈。它會執行，但不會從自己身上學習。

That memory has stayed with me, because the same pattern shows up in every operator conversation I have today. Most companies are like this. Most lives are too. And I think the next five years will sort companies into two piles based on a single property: whether their loops actually close. 這段記憶一直跟著我，因為同樣的模式出現在我今天與經營者的每一場對話裡。大多數公司是這樣，大多數人生也是。而我認為，接下來五年會依據單一性質把公司分成兩堆：它們的迴圈到底閉不閉合。

What an open loop is, and what a closed loop is｜什麼是開迴圈，什麼是閉迴圈

An open loop runs forward and never turns around. You decide. You do. You half-observe what happened. You start the next cycle with most of the lesson already gone. 開迴圈只往前跑，從不回頭。你決定、你執行、你半吊子地觀察發生了什麼，然後帶著大部分早已流失的教訓，開始下一個循環。

A closed loop turns around. You capture the input and the reasoning. You execute. You capture what shipped and what slipped. You measure the outcome against the intent. You feed the result back so the next cycle starts smarter than the last one. 閉迴圈會回頭。你記錄下輸入和推理，你執行，你記錄下什麼交付了、什麼延誤了，你拿結果對照原本的意圖來量測，然後把結果餵回去，讓下一個循環比上一個更聰明地起跑。

Open loops repeat. Closed loops compound. 開迴圈只是重複，閉迴圈才會複利。

If you only take one sentence from this post, take this one: if a process matters in your company, it should produce artifacts the company can learn from later. Without those artifacts, the process is open. It does not matter how earnest the retro is. 如果這篇文章你只帶走一句話，帶走這句：如果一個流程在你的公司裡很重要，它就應該產出「公司日後能從中學習的文件產物」。沒有那些產物，這個流程就是開的——無論你的回顧會開得多麼真誠。

Why almost every company is an open loop by default｜為什麼幾乎每家公司預設都是開迴圈

A normal decision in a normal company goes like this. Someone makes a call, often in a DM or a hallway. Work gets assigned, sometimes verbally. People execute. Status gets reported manually, usually in a meeting that nobody records. Outcomes are partially observed by the people closest to them. Lessons get inconsistently captured, often nowhere. The next cycle starts with most of that context gone. 一家正常公司裡的一個正常決策是這樣的：某人做了一個決定，通常是在私訊或走廊上。工作被指派下去，有時只是口頭。大家開始執行。進度靠人工回報，通常是在一場沒有人記錄的會議上。結果只被最靠近它的那些人片段地觀察到。教訓被零散地記下，往往根本沒被記下。下一個循環開始時，大部分脈絡都不見了。

Information loss happens at every step. The reasoning behind a decision dies in Slack. The tradeoffs you considered die in a 1:1. The customer context that shaped the spec dies in a sales call recording nobody re-watched. The reason a sprint slipped dies in three engineers’ heads. 每一步都在流失資訊。決策背後的推理死在 Slack 裡，你權衡過的取捨死在一對一會議裡，形塑了規格的客戶脈絡死在一段沒人重看的銷售通話錄音裡，一次衝刺延誤的原因死在三個工程師的腦袋裡。

By the time the next planning meeting arrives, the company is operating on memory, not data. And human memory is a famously bad database. 等到下一次規劃會議到來，公司靠的是記憶，不是資料。而人類記憶是一個出了名的爛資料庫。

This is why so many companies feel busy and slow at the same time. They are not slow because people are lazy. They are slow because every cycle pays a tax to reconstruct context that should have been captured the first time. 這就是為什麼這麼多公司同時感覺又忙又慢。它們慢不是因為人懶，而是因為每個循環都要繳一筆稅，去重建那些第一次就該被記錄下來的脈絡。

A lot of middle management exists to pay that tax on behalf of the rest of the company. People become the integration layer because the system is not one. That is an expensive way to run an org chart. 很多中階管理職的存在，就是代替公司其他人繳這筆稅。人變成了整合層，因為系統本身不是。用這種方式維持一張組織圖，代價很高。

Five places companies leak the loop｜公司最常在五個地方讓迴圈漏掉

If you are an operator and you want to find your own open loops fast, look here first. 如果你是經營者，想快速找出自己的開迴圈，先看這五個地方。

Decisions in DMs. The reasoning behind most important calls in your company is in private channels nobody can search. When the decision turns out to be wrong six months later, the reasoning is gone. You cannot learn from a decision you cannot reread. 私訊裡的決策。你公司裡大部分重要決定背後的推理，都躺在沒人搜尋得到的私人頻道裡。六個月後這個決定被證明是錯的，推理已經不見了。你無法從一個你重讀不到的決策裡學到東西。

Meetings without artifacts. Sales calls, design reviews, hiring debriefs, customer interviews. These happen, then evaporate. The team that did the meeting walks away with a vibe. Everyone else gets nothing. 沒有產出物的會議。銷售通話、設計評審、面試檢討、客戶訪談。這些事發生了，然後就蒸發了。開會的那組人帶走一種「感覺」，其他所有人什麼都沒拿到。

Roadmaps disconnected from outcomes. What shipped lives in one system. What moved the metric lives in another. Often nobody connects the two on purpose. So the company keeps shipping things and keeps being surprised by which ones mattered. 與結果脫節的路線圖。「出了什麼貨」住在一個系統，「什麼推動了指標」住在另一個系統，而通常沒有人刻意去把兩者接起來。於是公司持續出貨，也持續對「哪些真的有用」感到意外。

Customer signal stuck at the edges. Support hears the same complaint forty times. Sales hears the same objection in every demo. Product almost never finds out in a form they can act on. 卡在邊緣的客戶訊號。客服聽同一個抱怨聽了四十次，業務在每一場示範裡聽到同一個反對意見，而產品幾乎從來沒有以「可以據以行動的形式」得知這些。

Retros that do not change next sprint. Lessons get “captured” in a doc nobody opens before the next planning meeting. The lesson exists. It just does not enter the next cycle. 不會改變下一次衝刺的回顧會。教訓被「記錄」在一份沒人會在下次規劃會議前打開的文件裡。教訓確實存在，它只是沒有進入下一個循環。

What it takes to actually close one｜真正閉合一個迴圈需要什麼

A closed loop produces four artifacts every cycle. Not three. Not “the important ones.” All four. 一個閉迴圈每個循環會產出四份文件產物。不是三份，也不是「重要的那幾份」。四份，全部。

The input artifact: what you decided, with what context and what constraints. Written down somewhere durable. 輸入產物：你決定了什麼，基於什麼脈絡、受什麼限制。寫在一個能長久保存的地方。

The execution artifact: what shipped, what slipped, what changed mid-flight, who owned what. 執行產物：什麼交付了、什麼延誤了、途中什麼改變了、誰負責什麼。

The outcome artifact: what actually happened, measured against the original intent. Not what you hoped happened. 結果產物：實際發生了什麼，對照原本的意圖來量測。不是你希望發生了什麼。

The improvement artifact: what specifically changes in the next cycle because of this one. Not “we should be better at planning.” Something concrete you can point at. 改進產物：因為這個循環，下一個循環具體會改變什麼。不是「我們規劃能力該加強」，而是某個你指得出來的具體東西。

If any of the four is missing, the loop is still open. 四份裡少了任何一份，迴圈就仍然是開的。

Why this is suddenly worth doing｜為什麼這件事突然變得值得做

Closed loops were always the right design. For most of my career they were just too expensive to run. 閉迴圈一直都是正確的設計。在我職涯的大部分時間裡，它只是貴到跑不起來。

Capturing meetings, summarizing calls, reconciling specs against shipped code, tying customer signals to roadmap items, reading last quarter’s retros before this quarter’s planning. This was a full-time job per loop. Most companies hired a chief of staff to do a fraction of it. 記錄會議、摘要通話、把規格跟已交付的程式碼對帳、把客戶訊號連到路線圖項目、在這一季規劃前先讀上一季的回顧——每一個迴圈都是一份全職工作。大多數公司請一個幕僚長，也只能做到其中一小部分。

LLMs collapse the cost of synthesis to near zero. Recordings become structured summaries. Tickets and PRs become a queryable history. Customer tickets become trend reports. Old sprints become advice on this sprint’s plan. LLM 把「綜整」的成本壓縮到趨近於零。錄音變成結構化摘要，工單和 PR 變成可查詢的歷史，客戶工單變成趨勢報告，舊的衝刺變成對本次衝刺計畫的建議。

This is why I think the AI bill is the wrong thing to be optimizing right now. The AI bill is the price of finally running closed loops at company scale. Cutting it to look efficient is like cutting electricity to save on lighting. You are not saving money. You are turning the lights off. 所以我認為，現在把 AI 帳單當成最佳化目標是搞錯了方向。那筆帳單，是你終於能在公司規模上跑閉迴圈所付的價錢。為了看起來有效率而砍它，就像為了省電費而斷電——你不是在省錢，你是在關燈。

Context is the new management layer. The more an agent knows about your company, the more loops it can close that no human had time to close before. 脈絡就是新的管理層。一個代理對你公司知道得越多，它就能閉合越多過去沒有任何人有時間閉合的迴圈。

One closed loop, in detail｜一個閉迴圈的細節

Take sprint planning. Probably the most expensive open loop in any product company. 以衝刺規劃為例。這大概是任何產品公司裡最昂貴的一個開迴圈。

The open loop version is the one you already know. PM guesses scope. Engineering commits. Half ships, sometimes less. A retro maybe happens. Lessons go in a doc. Next sprint starts from a blank page and a hopeful estimate. 開迴圈版本你已經很熟了：PM 猜範圍，工程承諾，一半交付出去，有時更少。回顧會可能有開。教訓進了一份文件。下一次衝刺從一張白紙和一個充滿希望的估算重新開始。

The closed loop version looks like this. An agent has standing access to Linear, GitHub, the engineering Slack channel, customer support tickets, sales call transcripts, last sprint’s plan, and the actual ship log. Before planning, it produces a brief. What was planned versus shipped. Which work slipped, and why. Which shipped items mapped to a real customer pain in support tickets. Who is overloaded. Where ownership is unclear. Which decisions are blocking work nobody is escalating. 閉迴圈版本長這樣：一個代理常駐存取 Linear、GitHub、工程部門的 Slack 頻道、客服工單、銷售通話逐字稿、上次衝刺的計畫，以及實際的交付紀錄。在規劃之前，它產出一份簡報：計畫了什麼 vs. 交付了什麼；哪些工作延誤了，為什麼；哪些交付項目對應到客服工單裡真實的客戶痛點；誰的負擔過重；哪裡的權責不清；哪些決定正在阻塞工作，卻沒有任何人上報。

The planning meeting starts from that brief, not a blank doc. The PM is no longer guessing. The engineers are no longer reconstructing. The conversation moves immediately to judgment, not status. 規劃會議從那份簡報開始，而不是一份空白文件。PM 不再是在猜，工程師不再是在重建脈絡。對話立刻進入判斷，而不是進度回報。

After the sprint, the agent measures the brief’s predictions against reality. The estimates that were wrong get tagged. The work types that consistently slip get flagged. The next brief is sharper. Two quarters in, planning takes half the time and the plans are closer to what actually ships. 衝刺結束後，代理拿簡報裡的預測跟現實比對。估錯的部分被標記起來，總是延誤的工作類型被標示出來。下一份簡報就更銳利。兩季之後，規劃只花一半時間，而計畫也更接近實際交付出來的東西。

What actually compounds｜真正會複利的是什麼

Open loop companies forget. Closed loop companies compound. 開迴圈的公司會遺忘，閉迴圈的公司會複利。

Open loop lives forget. Closed loop lives compound. 開迴圈的人生會遺忘，閉迴圈的人生會複利。

The companies that pull away over the next five years will not be the ones that “use AI” the most. Everyone uses AI now. The ones that pull away will be the ones whose loops actually close, where every cycle is sharper than the last because the artifacts from the last one are still in the room. 接下來五年拉開差距的公司，不會是「最會用 AI」的那些——現在人人都在用 AI。拉開差距的，會是那些迴圈真正閉合的公司：每一個循環都比上一個更銳利，因為上一個循環留下的文件產物還在這個房間裡。

This week, name one open loop in your company. Just one. Close it before you close any others. See what changes. 這禮拜，說出你公司裡的一個開迴圈。就一個。在閉合其他任何迴圈之前，先把它閉合。看看會有什麼改變。

Then look at your own life and pick one there too. 然後看看你自己的人生，也在那裡挑一個。

你的 AI 整合，還卡在 1992 年

Apr 20, 2026｜2026 年 4 月 20 日

I’ve been watching a Steve Jobs talk from 1992 and it explains most of what’s wrong with how people are using AI in 2026. 我最近在看一段 1992 年 Steve Jobs 的演講，它解釋了 2026 年人們使用 AI 的方式裡，大部分的毛病。

He gave it at MIT Sloan, after he’d left Apple and was running NeXT. He was citing Paul Strassmann, a guy who’d been CIO at Xerox, the Pentagon, and NASA, and had spent two decades studying where corporate IT actually paid off. 那場演講在 MIT 史隆管理學院，當時他已離開蘋果、正在經營 NeXT。他引用的是 Paul Strassmann——曾任全錄、五角大廈和 NASA 的資訊長，花了二十年研究企業 IT 究竟在哪裡真正產生回報。

The Two Columns｜兩個欄位

Strassmann had found something simple. Every company he looked at spent about 2% of revenue on IT. The number was roughly the same whether the company was winning or losing. What moved was what the tech was doing. Strassmann 發現了一件很單純的事：他研究的每一家公司，都把大約 2% 的營收花在 IT 上。無論這家公司是贏家還是輸家，這個數字都差不多。真正有差別的，是這些科技在做什麼。

Some companies pointed the 2% at individual work. Tools that made one person’s day faster. Word processors, spreadsheets, email. The memo still got written. It just got written in an hour instead of three. Strassmann called this management productivity, but the simpler way to say it is that the tech was speeding up the people. 有些公司把那 2% 指向個人工作：讓一個人的一天更快的工具——文書處理、試算表、電子郵件。備忘錄還是得寫，只是從三小時縮短成一小時。Strassmann 稱之為「管理生產力」，但更簡單的講法是：這些科技在加速「人」。

Other companies pointed the 2% at the business itself. Tech that shortened the cycle that made the money. Catch the stockout before it stocks out. Push the invoice the day the work finishes. Reprice the product the hour demand shifts. This is operational productivity, or just: the tech was speeding up the business. 另一些公司把那 2% 指向生意本身：縮短那個「賺錢的循環」的科技。在缺貨發生前就攔截缺貨；工作完成當天就把發票推出去；需求變動的那一小時就重新定價。這叫「營運生產力」，或者更直白地說：這些科技在加速「生意」。

Same budget. Same era. The winners pointed the tech at the business. The losers pointed it at the people, got faster workers, and wondered why the P&L didn’t move. 同樣的預算，同樣的年代。贏家把科技指向生意，輸家把它指向人，換來一批更快的員工，然後納悶損益表為什麼沒動。

Swap “PC” For “AI”｜把「個人電腦」換成「AI」

Now do the substitution. The whole talk plays. 現在做這個替換，整場演講照樣成立。

A lot of the 2026 conversation is sloppy about this because it mistakes form factor for direction. The popular take is that copilots are old, agents are new, ship the agents. As if the shape of the thing decides where it’s pointed. It doesn’t. Agents can be pointed at the person or at the business. Most of them are pointed at the person. 2026 年的討論在這件事上很草率，因為它把「形式」誤認成「方向」。流行的說法是：copilot 過時了、代理才是新的，快出代理吧——彷彿東西的形狀決定了它指向哪裡。並沒有。代理可以指向人，也可以指向生意，而大部分都指向了人。

An agent that drafts your board update is pointed at you. An agent that triages your inbox is pointed at you. An agent that summarizes every call and files the notes is pointed at you. No matter how autonomous it is, if the thing it’s doing is making one person faster at their own work, it’s speeding up a person, not the business. 一個替你草擬董事會報告的代理，指向的是你。一個替你分類收件匣的代理，指向的是你。一個摘要每通電話並歸檔筆記的代理，指向的還是你。不管它有多自主，只要它做的事是讓一個人在自己的工作上變快，它加速的就是一個人，不是生意。

Faster People Is Not A Faster Business｜人變快，不等於生意變快

You might push back here. If the people inside my business get faster, isn’t the business faster too? No, and it’s worth knowing why. 你可能會反駁：如果我生意裡的人都變快了，生意不就跟著變快了嗎？不會，而且值得搞清楚為什麼。

A business cycle is mostly waiting, not working. The lead sits in an inbox for four hours before anyone sees it. The demo is three days out. The proposal waits two days for approval. The onboarding call is booked for next Tuesday. Most of the clock on any revenue cycle is idle time between handoffs, not working time on a task. 一個商業循環大部分是在「等」，不是在「做」。那筆潛在客戶在收件匣裡躺了四小時才有人看到；示範排在三天後；提案等兩天才簽核；上手通話約在下週二。任何營收循環的時鐘，大部分走在交接之間的閒置時間上，而不是走在任務上的工作時間。

AI pointed at a person shortens working time. The rep answers in thirty seconds instead of five minutes. AI pointed at the business removes waiting time between handoffs. The lead gets qualified and replied to in thirty seconds instead of four hours overnight. Compressing waiting is how cycles actually get shorter. Speeding up a person at a non-bottleneck step is a local optimization. It feels like progress on the Monday standup and doesn’t show up in the quarter. 指向人的 AI 縮短的是工作時間：業務代表從五分鐘縮短到三十秒回覆。指向生意的 AI 消除的是交接之間的等待時間：那筆潛在客戶從隔夜四小時，變成三十秒內完成資格判定並回覆。壓縮等待，才是循環真正縮短的方式。在非瓶頸環節上加速一個人，是局部最佳化——它在週一的站立會議上感覺像進展，卻不會出現在季報上。

What About Personal Productivity?｜那個人生產力呢？

Fair question. If I’m a solo operator, am I not the whole business? If I get faster, doesn’t the business get faster? 合理的問題。如果我是一人公司，我不就是整個生意嗎？我變快了，生意不就變快了？

Yes, if you’re truly solo. A one-person business has no handoffs. The person is the cycle. An agent that makes you reply faster is also an agent that shortens lead-to-response time. The two columns collapse into one because the person is the operation. 是的，如果你真的是一個人。一人公司沒有交接，這個人就是整個循環。一個讓你回覆更快的代理，同時也是一個縮短「潛客到回應」時間的代理。兩個欄位塌縮成一個，因為這個人就是整套營運。

But the moment anyone else is in the loop, they separate again. A VA, a contractor, a part-time editor, a freelancer. Once there are handoffs, the wait time between them is where the clock lives. Speeding up your own work while the handoffs stay slow is the same old trap at small scale. 但只要迴圈裡多了任何一個人，兩者就又分開了。一個虛擬助理、一個外包、一個兼職編輯、一個自由工作者。一旦有了交接，時鐘就走在交接之間的等待時間裡。在交接依然緩慢的情況下加速自己的工作，只是同一個老陷阱的小尺寸版本。

There’s also a simpler case. You just want your work to feel better. You want your inbox less painful. You want your deck to write itself. That’s fine. Just don’t pretend you’re transforming the business when you’re adopting a personal tool. Both can be true. They’re just different things. Buy the personal tool, use it happily, and point a different agent at the loop. 還有一種更單純的情況：你只是希望工作起來舒服一點。你希望收件匣不那麼痛苦，希望簡報自己會寫。這完全沒問題。只是別在採用一個個人工具時，假裝自己正在轉型整個生意。兩件事可以同時成立，它們只是不同的東西。買那個個人工具、開心地用它，然後另外派一個代理去指向那個循環。

The mistake isn’t pointing AI at yourself. The mistake is pointing all of your AI at yourself and calling it strategy. 錯的不是把 AI 指向自己。錯的是把你「所有的」AI 都指向自己，然後管它叫策略。

Size Is Not The Tell｜規模不是判準

Size isn’t the way to sort this either. A short agent that watches the support inbox and pings the success team the moment a high-value account says something churn-like is pointed at the business. A small script that catches the pricing error before the quote leaves the office is pointed at the business. One well-placed thing at one critical step can shorten the revenue cycle by a day. 規模也不是分辨的方式。一個短短的代理，盯著客服信箱，在高價值客戶說出任何像是要流失的話時立刻通知客戶成功團隊——它指向的是生意。一個小腳本，在報價單離開公司前攔下定價錯誤——它指向的也是生意。在一個關鍵環節上擺對一件東西，就能把營收循環縮短一天。

The question isn’t how big the agent is. It’s what gets faster when it runs, the person or the business. 問題不是這個代理有多大，而是它跑起來時，變快的是人，還是生意。

The Monday Audit｜週一稽核

If you want to run this on your own stack, don’t count your agents. Map them. 如果你想在自己的技術棧上跑一次這個檢查，別去數你的代理，去畫出它們的位置。

Sketch how revenue actually moves in your business. Lead in, qualified, demo, close, onboarded, renewed. Or idea in, made, shipped, bought, supported. Whatever your cycle is. Mark the handoffs, not just the tasks. Most of the clock lives between the boxes, not inside them. 畫出營收在你生意裡實際流動的路徑：潛客進來、判定資格、示範、成交、上手、續約。或者：點子進來、做出來、出貨、被購買、被支援。不管你的循環長什麼樣。標出「交接」，不要只標任務。時鐘大部分走在方塊「之間」，不是方塊「裡面」。

Now look at every AI integration you’ve shipped. For each one, ask one question. When this runs, does the business move faster, or does one person get faster at their own work? 現在檢視你已上線的每一個 AI 整合。對每一個問一個問題：當它執行時，是生意跑得更快，還是某一個人在自己的工作上變快了？

Then ask it of the whole stack. If you unplugged every AI integration tomorrow, would the cycle stretch out, or would a few people just type more? 接著對整個技術棧問一次：如果明天你把所有 AI 整合都拔掉，是循環會被拉長，還是只是幾個人得多打點字？

Most operators I’ve run this with realize their agents are almost entirely pointed at people. A few realize it’s a hundred percent. The number is rarely below sixty. 跟我一起跑過這個檢查的經營者，大多數都發現自己的代理幾乎全部指向人。少數人發現是百分之百。這個數字很少低於六成。

The reason this keeps happening is that it’s an attention problem, not a technology problem. Pointing an agent at yourself feels like action. You ship it, you see it working, the demo lands. Pointing an agent at the business feels like work. You have to sit with how the business actually runs, figure out where the wait time lives, and wire something into it you trust enough to let run. 這件事一再發生，是因為它是注意力問題，不是技術問題。把代理指向自己感覺像在行動：你上線它、你看見它在動、示範很成功。把代理指向生意則感覺像在做苦工：你得坐下來面對生意實際如何運轉、搞清楚等待時間住在哪裡，然後把某個你信任到願意放它自己跑的東西接進去。

The Trap Is Older Than AI｜這個陷阱比 AI 老得多

The split isn’t an AI thing. Strassmann drew it studying mainframes in the seventies. Jobs translated it to PCs in 1992. We’re living it in AI in 2026. Three eras. Three technologies. Same split. Each time new tech shows up, a loud generation of tools points at the person and a quieter generation points at the business. Person tools get the press. Business tools get the returns. 這個分野不是 AI 專屬的。Strassmann 在七〇年代研究大型主機時就畫出了它，Jobs 在 1992 年把它翻譯成個人電腦版，我們則在 2026 年用 AI 重演一遍。三個年代、三種科技、同一個分野。每次新科技出現，都會有喧鬧的一代工具指向人，以及安靜的一代工具指向生意。指向人的工具拿到版面，指向生意的工具拿到回報。

The question in 2026 isn’t whether you’re shipping agents. Everyone is. The question is whether your business moves any faster because of it. 2026 年的問題不是你有沒有在出代理——每個人都在出。問題是：你的生意有因此跑得更快嗎？

If you run the audit this week and the business isn’t any faster, that’s fine. Most of us started there. It just means the transformation you think you’re doing hasn’t started yet. The tech is new. The trap is 1992. 如果你這禮拜跑完稽核，發現生意並沒有變快，沒關係，我們大多數人都是從那裡開始的。那只代表你以為自己在做的轉型，其實還沒開始。科技是新的，陷阱是 1992 年的。

AI Made You More Productive. The Question Is: For Whom?

AI 讓你更有生產力了。問題是：為了誰？

Mar 13, 2026｜2026 年 3 月 13 日

A few weeks ago I wrote about Parkinson’s Law and AI - how we fill saved time with more work unless we actively protect it. 幾週前我寫過帕金森定律與 AI——除非我們主動保護，否則省下來的時間都會被更多工作填滿。

One reply stuck with me: “Leon, the problem isn’t me. It’s my company. I finished my work in 4 hours. My reward was more work.” 有一則回覆一直留在我心裡：「Leon，問題不在我，在我公司。我四小時就做完了工作，我得到的獎賞是更多工作。」

And I sat with that. Because they’re right. But they’re also only seeing half the picture. 我坐下來好好想了這件事。因為他說得對，但他也只看到了一半。

Here’s the uncomfortable truth nobody wants to say out loud: AI created a massive productivity surplus. And now there’s a fight over who gets to keep it. 以下是沒人想大聲說出口的不舒服真相：AI 創造了巨大的生產力盈餘，而現在正在打一場「誰能留下它」的仗。

The Company’s Side (And They’re Not Wrong)｜公司這一方（而且他們沒有錯）

Amazon cut 30,000 corporate roles in four months. CEO Andy Jassy said AI would “reshape operations and reduce the need for some corporate roles.” Block slashed 40% of its workforce - over 4,000 people. Jack Dorsey’s explanation was blunt: “A significantly smaller team, using these tools, can achieve more.” Amazon 在四個月內裁掉 30,000 個企業職位。執行長 Andy Jassy 說 AI 會「重塑營運，並降低對某些企業職位的需求」。Block 砍掉 40% 的員工——超過 4,000 人。Jack Dorsey 的解釋很直白：「一支顯著更小的團隊，用上這些工具，能達成更多。」

And if you run a company, this math is hard to argue with. 而如果你在經營一家公司，這筆帳很難反駁。

If a team of 10 can now produce what used to take 15, why keep 15? That’s not cruelty. That’s the same logic every business has followed since the invention of the assembly line. New technology creates surplus. Organizations capture it. 如果 10 個人現在能產出過去要 15 個人才做得出來的東西，為什麼還要留 15 個？這不是殘忍，這是自從裝配線發明以來每一門生意都遵循的同一套邏輯：新科技創造盈餘，組織把它捕獲。

This is what companies do. They’re not going to look at a 40% productivity gain and say “great, everyone go home at 2pm.” They’re going to say “great, we need fewer people” or “great, now this team can take on the project we couldn’t afford before.” 這就是公司會做的事。他們不會看著 40% 的生產力提升然後說「太好了，大家兩點就下班吧」。他們會說「太好了，我們需要的人變少了」，或是「太好了，這個團隊現在可以接下我們以前負擔不起的那個專案」。

UC Berkeley researchers tracked about 200 employees at a tech company for eight months. The ones who adopted AI expanded scope, took on tasks they used to outsource, blurred work-life boundaries. But here’s the part people miss - the environment drove much of that expansion. When you finish faster, expectations adjust. Not through some official policy. Just… naturally. Deadlines tighten. Scope grows. “You can handle this now, right?” 柏克萊的研究者追蹤了一家科技公司約 200 名員工八個月。那些採用 AI 的人擴大了工作範圍、接下以前會外包的任務、工作與生活的界線變得模糊。但大家忽略的部分是——這種擴張大多是環境驅動的。當你完成得更快，期待就會調整；不是透過什麼正式政策，就只是……自然而然。截止日變緊，範圍變大，「這個你現在應付得來吧？」

The organization absorbs the gain. That’s not a bug. That’s how organizations work. 組織吸收了那份增益。這不是 bug，這就是組織運作的方式。

The Individual’s Side (And They’re Not Wrong Either)｜個人這一方（而他們也沒有錯）

But if you’re the person doing the work, this deal feels terrible. 但如果你是那個實際做事的人，這筆交易感覺糟透了。

You learned the tools. You got faster. You upskilled on your own time. And your reward is… the same salary, more work, and the vague threat that if you don’t keep accelerating, someone cheaper (or no one at all) will replace you. 你學了工具、你變快了、你用自己的時間升級技能。而你的獎賞是……一樣的薪水、更多的工作，外加一個模糊的威脅：如果你不繼續加速，某個更便宜的人（或者根本沒有人）就會取代你。

ActivTrak analyzed 164,000 workers and found that after AI adoption, focused deep work actually dropped. What went up? Coordination. Messaging. Context-switching. People produced more but spent their “saved” time managing the increased volume. ActivTrak 分析了 164,000 名工作者，發現採用 AI 之後，專注的深度工作反而下降了。上升的是什麼？協調、傳訊、情境切換。人們產出變多，卻把「省下來」的時間拿去管理暴增的工作量。

Upwork found 77% of professionals reporting increased workload despite speed gains. Not because they chose more work. Because the system metabolized their efficiency before they could. Upwork 發現 77% 的專業工作者回報工作量增加，儘管速度確實提升了。不是因為他們選擇了更多工作，而是因為系統在他們反應過來之前，就把他們的效率代謝掉了。

So you’re faster, busier, more mentally loaded, and now you’re also reading headlines about AI replacing your job entirely. The individual’s rational response is obvious: capture the gains yourself. Work less. Automate quietly. Don’t advertise how fast you’ve become. Because the moment you do, the surplus gets claimed. 所以你更快、更忙、心理負荷更重，現在還要一邊讀著「AI 將完全取代你的工作」的頭條。個人的理性反應很明顯：自己把增益留下來。少做一點，安靜地自動化，別張揚你變得多快——因為你一張揚，那份盈餘就會被認領走。

The Dilemma Nobody’s Solving｜沒有人在解的兩難

This is a genuine dilemma. Not a problem with a clean answer. 這是一個真正的兩難，不是一個有乾淨答案的問題。

The company is right that capturing productivity gains is how businesses survive and grow. Every major economic shift - mechanization, computers, the internet - played out this way. The surplus got absorbed into growth, lower costs, or headcount reduction. Expecting AI to be different is naive. 公司是對的：捕獲生產力增益正是企業存活與成長的方式。每一次重大的經濟轉變——機械化、電腦、網際網路——都是這樣演的。盈餘被吸收進成長、更低的成本，或人力精簡裡。期待 AI 會有所不同，是天真的。

The individual is right that they’re the ones who developed the capability, and watching all the benefits flow upward while the workload flows down is demoralizing. Especially when “upskilling” was supposed to be the answer to job security, and now upskilling just means doing three people’s jobs. 個人也是對的：能力是他們自己養出來的，而眼看著好處全部往上流、工作量全部往下流，非常令人洩氣。尤其當「升級技能」原本應該是工作保障的答案，如今升級技能卻只意味著做三個人的工作。

Neither side is being greedy. Both are being rational. And that’s what makes this hard. 雙方都不是在貪婪，雙方都是在理性行事。而這正是它難的地方。

What History Tells Us (It’s Not Comforting)｜歷史怎麼說（不太安慰人）

Every major productivity technology has created this exact tension. And historically, the organization wins first. 每一次重大的生產力科技都製造出完全相同的張力。而歷史上，組織總是先贏。

The industrial revolution made workers dramatically more productive. Factory owners captured the surplus for decades - longer hours, lower per-unit wages, child labor - before unions, regulation, and social pressure redistributed some of the gains back to workers. That redistribution took 50-80 years. 工業革命讓工人的生產力大幅提升。工廠主捕獲了這份盈餘數十年——更長的工時、更低的單位工資、童工——直到工會、法規和社會壓力把部分增益重新分配回工人身上。那次重分配花了五十到八十年。

Computers made office workers more productive starting in the 1980s. Companies captured it through headcount efficiency. The “productivity paradox” of that era was that GDP rose but wages didn’t. It took until the late 1990s for workers to see meaningful wage gains from the computer revolution. 電腦從一九八〇年代開始提升辦公室工作者的生產力，公司透過人力效率把它捕獲。那個年代的「生產力悖論」是：GDP 上升了，薪資沒有。工作者要等到九〇年代末，才從電腦革命中看到有意義的薪資成長。

The pattern is consistent: technology creates surplus, organizations capture it first, individuals eventually claw some back through market dynamics, regulation, or cultural shifts. But “eventually” can mean a generation. 模式是一致的：科技創造盈餘，組織先捕獲它，個人最終透過市場動態、法規或文化轉變討回一部分。但「最終」可能是一整個世代那麼久。

So Who Wins With AI?｜那麼在 AI 這一局，誰會贏？

I think it depends on what kind of work you do. 我認為這取決於你做的是哪一種工作。

If your job is primarily execution - producing deliverables that AI can now produce faster or cheaper - the organization will capture most of the surplus. This is already happening. The layoffs aren’t rumors anymore. 如果你的工作主要是執行——產出那些 AI 現在能做得更快或更便宜的交付物——組織會捕獲大部分盈餘。這已經在發生了，裁員不再是傳聞。

If your job is primarily judgment - deciding what to build, which problems matter, how to navigate ambiguity - you’re in a stronger position. AI amplifies your judgment instead of replacing it. And the surplus is harder for the organization to extract because it lives in your head, not in a workflow. 如果你的工作主要是判斷——決定要做什麼、哪些問題重要、如何在模糊中前行——你的位置比較穩。AI 放大你的判斷，而不是取代它。而且那份盈餘比較難被組織抽取，因為它住在你腦袋裡，不在某個工作流程裡。

The most interesting case is the middle. People whose jobs are a mix of execution and judgment. They’re faster at the execution part, which means they can spend more time on the judgment part. But only if they actively make that shift. If they just do more execution faster, the organization absorbs the gain and they become easier to replace. 最有趣的是中間那群人：工作是執行與判斷混合的人。他們在執行的部分變快了，這意味著他們可以把更多時間花在判斷的部分——但前提是他們主動做出這個轉移。如果他們只是更快地做更多執行，組織就會吸收那份增益，而他們變得更容易被取代。

I don’t have a grand theory. But I’ve been thinking about this as both a founder and an individual. 我沒有什麼宏大理論。但我一直同時以創辦人和個人的身分思考這件事。

As a founder, I’m trying to resist the default of just piling more work on a faster team. The temptation is real. “We can do more” is the most natural sentence in business. But I’ve burned out teams before, pre-AI, with exactly this logic. More capacity doesn’t mean more should get done. Sometimes it means the same things get done with more care, more thinking, more space. 身為創辦人，我試著抗拒那個預設反應：往一支變快的團隊身上堆更多工作。誘惑是真實的，「我們可以做更多」是商業世界裡最自然的一句話。但在 AI 之前，我就用完全相同的邏輯把團隊燒壞過。更多產能不代表就該完成更多事；有時候它代表的是：同樣的事，做得更用心、更多思考、更多餘裕。

As an individual, I’m deliberately shifting my time toward work AI can’t absorb yet - strategic decisions, relationship-building, the kind of thinking that requires sitting with ambiguity. Not because I’m noble. Because that’s where the leverage is. The execution layer is getting commoditized fast. 身為個人，我刻意把時間轉移到 AI 還吸收不了的工作上——策略決定、關係經營，以及那種需要與模糊共處的思考。不是因為我高尚，而是因為槓桿在那裡。執行層正在快速被商品化。

The honest answer is that this tension won’t resolve cleanly. Organizations will squeeze. Individuals will protect. The people who navigate it best won’t be the ones who pick a side - they’ll be the ones who understand both sides well enough to position themselves where the surplus is hardest to take from them. 誠實的答案是：這股張力不會乾淨地化解。組織會擠壓，個人會保護。最能在其中穿行的人，不會是選邊站的那些，而是把雙方都理解得夠透徹、因而能把自己放在「盈餘最難從他們身上被拿走」的位置上的那些人。

That means moving toward judgment. Toward the work that gets more valuable when AI handles the rest. 那意味著往判斷移動——往那些「當 AI 接手其餘一切時反而更值錢」的工作移動。

Not because it’s fair. Because it’s the game. 不是因為這樣才公平，而是因為這就是遊戲規則。

My Kid Doesn’t Need Instructions. He Needs Judgment.

我的孩子不需要說明書，他需要判斷力

Feb 18, 2026｜2026 年 2 月 18 日

My 12-year-old asked ChatGPT how to build a rubber band-powered racing car for his school project last week. It gave him a perfect step-by-step guide. Materials list, measurements, the physics behind stored elastic energy - the whole thing. 上週我十二歲的兒子為了學校作業，問 ChatGPT 怎麼做一台橡皮筋動力賽車。它給了他一份完美的逐步指南：材料清單、尺寸、彈性位能背後的物理——一應俱全。

He followed every instruction. Built it in an afternoon. It worked on the first try. 他照著每一條指示做，一個下午就完成了，而且第一次試就成功。

And I realized: that’s the problem. 然後我意識到：問題就在這裡。

The Instructions Era Is Over｜「說明書時代」結束了

For most of human history, the hard part was getting the instructions. You needed a teacher, a textbook, a mentor, an apprenticeship. Access to knowledge was the bottleneck. 在人類歷史的大部分時間裡，困難的是「取得說明」。你需要一位老師、一本教科書、一個導師、一段學徒生涯。取得知識的管道，就是瓶頸。

That bottleneck is gone. My 9-year-old can ask an AI to explain quantum physics at a level he understands. And it will. Patiently. With analogies. At 11pm on a Tuesday. 那個瓶頸消失了。我九歲的兒子可以要求 AI 用他聽得懂的程度解釋量子物理，而它真的會——有耐心地、用比喻地、在某個週二晚上十一點。

So what’s left? 那還剩下什麼？

Judgment. The thing no AI can hand you in a step-by-step guide. 判斷力。那個沒有任何 AI 能用逐步指南遞給你的東西。

Judgment is knowing which problem to solve in the first place. It’s knowing when the AI’s answer is technically correct but practically wrong. It’s knowing when to follow the instructions and when to throw them out. 判斷力是：一開始就知道該解哪一個問題；是知道什麼時候 AI 的答案技術上正確、實務上卻是錯的；是知道什麼時候該照著說明走，什麼時候該把它扔掉。

And I don’t think most of us - parents, teachers, schools - are preparing kids for this shift. 而我認為我們大多數人——家長、老師、學校——都沒有為這個轉變在準備孩子。

What I Got Wrong As A Parent｜我身為父親做錯的事

I caught myself doing something recently that bothered me. My older son was struggling with a decision about which extracurricular to commit to. And my instinct was to tell him the answer. 最近我抓到自己在做一件讓我不太舒服的事。我大兒子正為了「該投入哪個課外活動」猶豫不決，而我的直覺是直接告訴他答案。

“Do this one. Here’s why.” 「選這個。理由是這樣。」

That’s instructions. That’s me being the AI - giving him the optimal output based on my pattern-matching from decades of experience. 那就是說明書。那是我在扮演 AI——根據我數十年經驗的模式比對，給他一個最佳輸出。

But my experience is from a different world. I grew up in a world where you pick a lane, get good at it, and stay. That mental model doesn’t apply to a kid who’ll graduate into a world where AI can pick up any skill in seconds. The competitive advantage isn’t in what you know how to do. It’s in knowing what’s worth doing. 但我的經驗來自一個不同的世界。我成長於一個「選一條路、把它練好、然後待著」的世界。那個心智模型，不適用於一個即將畢業進入「AI 能在幾秒內學會任何技能」的世界的孩子。競爭優勢不在於你會做什麼，而在於你知道什麼值得做。

So I stopped myself. Instead of answering, I asked him questions. 所以我停了下來。我沒有回答，而是問他問題。

What do you actually enjoy about each one? Not what you think you should enjoy. What part do you look forward to? 每一個當中，你真正享受的是什麼？不是你「覺得自己應該」享受的部分。你會期待的是哪一段？

If you could only do one for the next year and no one would judge you either way, which would you pick? 如果接下來一年你只能做一個，而且不管選哪個都沒有人會評價你，你會選哪個？

What would you regret not trying? 哪一個是你沒去試會後悔的？

He sat with it. It was uncomfortable. He wanted me to just tell him. But he made a choice. His own choice, with his own reasoning. 他跟這些問題坐了一陣子。過程不舒服，他希望我直接告訴他。但他做出了選擇——他自己的選擇，帶著他自己的理由。

That’s judgment practice. And it’s harder than following instructions. 那就是判斷力的練習。而它比照著說明書做要難得多。

Here’s something I’ve been thinking about that applies to adults too. As we get older, we accumulate experience. And experience is supposed to be this great advantage - pattern recognition, intuition, “I’ve seen this before.” 以下這件事我一直在想，而且對大人同樣適用。隨著年紀增長，我們累積經驗。而經驗理應是一項巨大優勢——模式辨識、直覺、「這個我看過」。

But experience also calcifies. You stop considering options outside your patterns. You default to what worked last time instead of what might work better now. 但經驗也會鈣化。你不再考慮自己模式以外的選項，你預設採用「上次有效的」，而不是「現在可能更有效的」。

I see this in myself constantly. Running LifeHack for 20+ years means I have strong instincts about what content works, how to structure a team, how to launch a product. But some of those instincts are from 2015. The world has shifted underneath them. 我不斷在自己身上看到這一點。經營 LifeHack 二十多年，意味著我對「什麼內容有效、團隊怎麼編組、產品怎麼上市」有著強烈直覺。但其中有些直覺來自 2015 年，而世界已經在它們腳下移動過了。

AI doesn’t have this problem. Ask it for ideas and it doesn’t care about your track record. It generates options you’d never consider because it has no ego invested in what worked before. AI 沒有這個問題。向它要點子，它不在乎你的過往戰績。它會生出你永遠不會考慮的選項，因為它對「以前什麼有效」沒有投入任何自尊。

The question is whether you have the judgment to evaluate those options honestly. Or whether your experience will make you dismiss the unfamiliar ones automatically. 問題在於，你有沒有判斷力去誠實地評估那些選項——還是你的經驗會讓你自動把陌生的那些駁回。

This is the skill I want my kids to develop. Not experience - they’ll get that naturally. Judgment. The ability to weigh options without defaulting to comfort. 這就是我希望孩子培養的能力。不是經驗——經驗他們自然會有。是判斷力：在不預設回到舒適圈的前提下權衡選項的能力。

You can’t sit a kid down and teach judgment the way you teach multiplication. But you can create conditions where it develops. 你沒辦法像教乘法那樣，把孩子按在椅子上教他判斷力。但你可以創造讓它長出來的條件。

Let them make real decisions with real consequences. Not catastrophic ones. But real. My 12-year-old manages his own schedule for the week. If he doesn’t allocate enough time for homework, he feels it. I don’t rescue him. 讓他們做有真實後果的真實決定。不是災難性的，但要是真實的。我十二歲的兒子自己安排一週的行程。如果他沒留夠時間寫作業，他會親身感受到後果。我不出手救他。

But because the act of formulating your own answer - even a wrong one - builds the muscle. 用問題取代答案。當我任一個兒子問我「我該怎麼做」時，我的第一個回應現在幾乎都是「你覺得呢？」不是為了討人厭，而是因為「自己組出一個答案」這個動作——哪怕是錯的答案——會長肌肉。

Let them use AI, then critique it. This one surprised me. I let my older son use ChatGPT for research, but then we talk about what it got wrong or what it left out. He’s starting to develop a sense for when AI outputs sound right but miss something important. That’s judgment. 讓他們用 AI，然後批評它。這一點讓我意外。我讓大兒子用 ChatGPT 做研究，但事後我們會討論它哪裡弄錯了、漏掉了什麼。他開始培養出一種感覺：什麼時候 AI 的輸出聽起來很對，卻漏掉了某個重要的東西。那就是判斷力。

Model uncertainty. I tell my kids when I don’t know the answer. When I’m making a decision I’m not sure about. When I changed my mind about something. Kids who see adults being certain about everything learn to fake certainty. Kids who see adults wrestling with ambiguity learn to sit with it. 示範不確定。我會告訴孩子我什麼時候不知道答案、什麼時候在做一個我沒把握的決定、什麼時候我改變了想法。看著大人對每件事都很篤定的孩子，學會的是假裝篤定；看著大人與模糊搏鬥的孩子，學會的是與模糊共處。

The Real Question For The AI Era｜AI 時代真正的問題

Everyone’s asking “what should we teach kids now that AI exists?” And the answers are usually some version of creativity, critical thinking, emotional intelligence. Which is fine. But vague. 每個人都在問「既然有了 AI，我們現在該教孩子什麼？」而答案通常是創造力、批判性思考、情緒智商的某種版本。這些都沒錯，但很空泛。

I think the answer is simpler and harder. 我認為答案更簡單，也更難。

Teach them to make decisions without complete information. Because that’s what judgment is. And that’s the one thing AI will never do for them - not because it can’t generate a decision, but because the whole point is that they need to own it. 教他們在資訊不完整的情況下做決定。因為那就是判斷力。而那是 AI 永遠不會替他們做的一件事——不是因為它產不出一個決定，而是因為整件事的重點就在於：那個決定必須是他們自己扛的。

AI can give you the options. AI can model the outcomes. AI can even recommend the “optimal” choice. AI 可以給你選項，AI 可以模擬結果，AI 甚至可以推薦那個「最佳」選擇。

But the kid who learns to sit with uncertainty, weigh tradeoffs against their own values, and commit to a choice they made themselves - that kid has something AI can’t replicate. 但一個學會與不確定共處、依據自己的價值觀權衡取捨、並為自己做出的選擇負起責任的孩子——那個孩子擁有 AI 複製不了的東西。

My rubber band car-building 12-year-old doesn’t need more instructions. He’s drowning in instructions. What he needs is the confidence to decide which instructions are worth following. Which means letting him get it wrong sometimes. That part is harder for me than for him. 這意味著要讓他偶爾做錯。而那部分，對我比對他更難。

That’s a real computer science problem. Algorithmic map generation. And he had to figure out how to describe it to the AI precisely enough to get a working result. 那是一個真正的計算機科學問題：演算法式地圖生成。而他必須想辦法把它描述得夠精確，才能讓 AI 給出可用的結果。

It took him a bunch of rounds going back and forth. “No, I want the walls to be random but not blocking the path.” “The tanks should start far apart.” “There should be places to hide but not too many.” Each time the AI tried something, he’d test it, see what was wrong, and try to explain the fix. 他來回折騰了好幾輪。「不對，我要牆是隨機的，但不能擋住通路。」「戰車一開始要離得遠一點。」「要有地方可以躲，但不能太多。」每次 AI 試了什麼，他就測試、看哪裡不對，然後試著把修正的方向解釋清楚。

He got there eventually. The maps aren’t perfect. But they work. And he understands what procedural generation is now - not because someone taught him the concept, but because he needed it for his game. 他最後做到了。地圖不完美，但可以用。而且他現在懂什麼是程序生成——不是因為誰教了他這個概念，而是因為他的遊戲需要它。

My 9-year-old’s project was more ambitious than I expected. He wanted a 3D first-person game where you escape from a prison. He had the whole thing in his head - the layout of the prison, the guards’ patrol routes, the items you need to find, the plot twists between chapters. 九歲那個的專案比我預期的更有野心。他想做一款 3D 第一人稱的越獄遊戲。整個東西都在他腦袋裡——監獄的格局、警衛的巡邏路線、你必須找到的道具、章節之間的劇情轉折。

The 3D graphics ended up looking like Roblox, which he was thrilled about. Three playable chapters. A storyline that actually holds together. 3D 畫面最後看起來像 Roblox，這讓他超級興奮。三個可玩的章節，一條真的接得起來的劇情線。

He’s nine. 他九歲。

The Real Bottleneck｜真正的瓶頸

Here’s what surprised me. The technology wasn’t the hard part. The AI could generate code, create game mechanics, design levels, render 3D environments. The tools are there. 讓我意外的是：技術不是困難的部分。AI 能生成程式碼、做出遊戲機制、設計關卡、算出 3D 場景。工具都到位了。

What was hard - for both of them - was articulating what was in their head. 真正困難的——對他們兩個都一樣——是把腦袋裡的東西講出來。

My son knew exactly what he wanted the random maps to feel like. He could picture it. He just couldn’t describe it in words precise enough for the AI to build it. There’s a huge gap between “I want it to be kind of random but also fair” and a specification the AI can execute on. 我兒子非常清楚他要那些隨機地圖「感覺起來」是什麼樣，他腦中有畫面。他只是沒辦法用夠精確的文字描述出來，讓 AI 據以打造。從「我要它有點隨機但又要公平」到一份 AI 能執行的規格之間，隔著一道巨大的鴻溝。

My 9-year-old had an even bigger version of this problem. He had a rich, detailed world in his imagination. Getting it out of his head and into language was the bottleneck. Not the coding. Not the technology. The translation from imagination to words. 九歲那個的這個問題更嚴重。他的想像裡有一個豐富而細緻的世界，把它從腦袋裡搬進語言，才是瓶頸。不是寫程式，不是技術，而是從想像到文字的那道翻譯。

I watched both of them develop this skill in real time. After a few sessions they were noticeably better at describing what they wanted. More specific. More structured in how they communicated. 我親眼看著他們兩個即時地長出這項能力。幾次之後，他們描述自己想要什麼的能力明顯變好了——更具體，表達方式也更有結構。

The Skill That Actually Matters｜真正重要的那項能力

We keep asking the wrong question about kids and AI. The question isn’t “should kids learn to code if AI can code for them?” That’s like asking whether kids should learn to drive when self-driving cars are coming. It misses the point. 關於孩子與 AI，我們一直在問錯問題。問題不是「如果 AI 會寫程式，孩子還該不該學寫程式？」那就像在問「自駕車要來了，孩子還該不該學開車？」——完全沒抓到重點。

The better question is: what does AI require you to be good at? 更好的問題是：AI 要求你擅長什麼？

And from watching my kids, the answer is clear. You need to be good at knowing what you want and explaining it clearly. 從觀察我的孩子來看，答案很清楚：你必須擅長「知道自己要什麼，並把它講清楚」。

That sounds simple. It isn’t. 這聽起來很簡單。並不簡單。

Think about how often you struggle to articulate an idea at work. How many meetings are just people failing to describe what they actually mean. How many projects go sideways because the spec was fuzzy. 想想你在工作上有多常說不清楚一個想法；有多少會議其實就是一群人在講不清楚自己真正的意思；有多少專案歪掉，是因為規格太模糊。

Now imagine a generation of kids who grew up practicing this skill - not in a classroom exercise, but because they needed it to build the thing they were excited about. 現在想像一整個世代的孩子，從小就在練這項能力——不是在課堂習題裡，而是因為他們需要它來做出自己興奮的東西。

My son didn’t learn “prompt engineering.” He learned how to take a fuzzy idea in his head and refine it into something precise enough to execute. That’s a thinking skill. It transfers to everything. 我兒子學到的不是「提示工程」。他學到的是如何把腦中一個模糊的想法，精煉成一個精確到足以被執行的東西。那是一項思考能力，它會遷移到所有事情上。

What I’d Tell Other Parents｜我會告訴其他家長什麼

You don’t need to be technical to do this with your kids. I didn’t sit down and teach them programming concepts. I showed them how to talk to the AI and then got out of the way. 你不需要懂技術，也能陪孩子做這件事。我沒有坐下來教他們程式設計概念，我只是示範怎麼跟 AI 說話，然後閃到一邊去。

The magic is that kids already have the creativity. They have worlds in their heads that are more imaginative than anything I’d come up with. What they need is the experience of translating those worlds into words. AI gives them a reason to practice that. 神奇之處在於，孩子本來就有創造力。他們腦袋裡的世界，比我想得出來的任何東西都更有想像力。他們需要的，是把那些世界翻譯成文字的經驗。而 AI 給了他們練習的理由。

A few things I noticed: 我觀察到幾件事：

The best prompts came from frustration. When the AI got something wrong, my kids had to figure out why it was wrong and explain the difference between what they got and what they wanted. That’s higher-order thinking dressed up as a game. 最好的提示來自挫折。當 AI 弄錯了什麼，我的孩子就得搞清楚它為什麼錯，並解釋「拿到的」和「想要的」之間差在哪裡。那是披著遊戲外衣的高階思考。

They learned iteration naturally. Not as a concept someone taught them, but as a thing you do. You try, it’s not right, you adjust, you try again. By the fifth or sixth round they stopped being frustrated by imperfect results and started treating each attempt as information. 他們自然而然學會了迭代。不是誰教的一個概念，而是一件你就這樣做的事：你試、不對、調整、再試。到了第五、六輪，他們不再為不完美的結果感到挫折，而開始把每一次嘗試都當成資訊。

They developed taste. My 9-year-old rejected several versions of his prison layout not because they were broken, but because they “didn’t feel right.” He was making design judgments. At nine. 他們養出了品味。我九歲的兒子否決了好幾版監獄格局，不是因為它們壞掉了，而是因為它們「感覺不對」。他在做設計判斷。九歲。

The Bigger Picture｜更大的圖像

I keep thinking about this: my kids built things last year that would have required a team of developers a decade ago. Not perfect versions - but working versions. Playable games with real mechanics and narratives. 我一直在想這件事：我的孩子去年做出來的東西，十年前需要一整支開發團隊。不是完美的版本，但是能跑的版本——有真實機制和敘事、可以玩的遊戲。

The barrier to building just dropped to zero. The only barrier left is: can you describe what you want to build? 「動手做」的門檻剛剛降到了零。唯一剩下的門檻是：你描述得出你想做什麼嗎？

That’s not a technology problem. That’s a clarity-of-thinking problem. And the best way I’ve found to practice it is to give a kid an AI and something they’re excited about building. 那不是技術問題，那是「思考是否清晰」的問題。而我找到最好的練習方式，就是給孩子一個 AI，加上一件他們興奮想做的東西。

The prison break game has bugs. The tank maps sometimes generate weird layouts. But my kids built them. And more importantly, they learned how to think clearly enough to explain what they imagined. 那款越獄遊戲有 bug，戰車地圖偶爾會生出奇怪的佈局。但那是我的孩子做出來的。更重要的是，他們學會了把思路理清到足以解釋自己想像的程度。

That might be the most useful skill they develop this year. 那可能是他們今年培養出來最有用的能力。
